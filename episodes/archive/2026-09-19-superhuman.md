September nineteenth, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Robots now have their own ambulance services.

Figure AI released Helix two point five, a model that let its humanoid robots walk into thirty homes they had never seen before and get to work immediately, tidying living rooms, folding and storing towels, and making beds. Generalizing to an unfamiliar environment is one of the hardest problems in robotics, which is what makes zero shot performance at that scale worth noticing.

Agility unveiled Digit five, which it calls the first truly safe humanoid worker. It detects people nearby and slows, stops, or lowers itself and shuts off its motors if someone approaches, which removes the need for safety barriers around it. Agility reports three hundred million dollars in multiyear orders and plans a North American launch in early twenty twenty seven, with Europe and the United Kingdom later that year.

A Tokyo startup called MW is building homes fitted with robotic arms that travel along ceiling mounted rails, an Iron Man inspired design that can carry groceries, separate pantry items from refrigerated goods, and fold laundry. The bet is that fixed track robots are safer and better suited to compact Japanese homes than two legged humanoids. MW plans to sell its first robot equipped home in twenty twenty eight.

UBTECH opened a plant in Liuzhou, the first in the world built to manufacture humanoid robots at ten thousand unit scale, with robots on the line helping to assemble more robots. Each unit gets over four hours of testing before it goes into an automated warehouse. UBTECH credits China's deep supply chain for making that kind of mass production possible.

The Japanese company GMO unveiled what it calls a humanoid ambulance, a repair van stocked with diagnostic tools, spare parts, and a backup humanoid ready to swap in when one breaks down mid shift. Engineers haul the broken robot back to base while the work keeps going. It is an early look at the support infrastructure a robot workforce would actually need.

A Waymo in San Francisco flagged two juvenile passengers handling a loaded AR style rifle inside the cab, and the company alerted police. It is not the first time one has reported its riders; another car tipped off San Mateo police about teens drinking and firing toy blasters. As robotaxis multiply, their sensors are becoming a new kind of authority in the back seat.

Casio's Moflin is a four hundred twenty nine dollar furry robot pet about the size of a hamster. It uses emotional recognition to develop its own personality over a period of weeks, which Casio says allows for more than four million possible emotional profiles. It cannot walk, but it responds to touch and sound with nuzzles, head tilts and chirps.

A glitch sent a dozen Coco delivery robots swarming a sidewalk near Lincoln Park in Chicago, boxing in pedestrians with nowhere to walk around them until the company caught the problem minutes later. It follows earlier incidents that sent a resident to the hospital and got the robots banned from parts of the city.

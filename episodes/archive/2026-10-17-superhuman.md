October seventeenth, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Robot helps paralyzed woman walk again.

This is Superhuman's Saturday Robotics Special. First, UCLA scientists unveiled PhoneBot, a nineteen inch walking robot that uses an Android phone as its torso, tapping the phone's camera, motion sensors and processor to see, balance and move. Beyond the phone, the parts cost about four hundred dollars, giving enthusiasts an affordable entry point into robotics.

Next, Jessica Tawil, a Lebanese influencer left paralyzed at sixteen in a car accident, posted a video of herself taking assisted steps with a robotic exoskeleton, her first time standing on her own feet in a decade. The video is blowing up on social media. The device mechanically supports her movement and helps her walk upright, and Superhuman calls it a sign that assistive robotics is reaching everyday life.

Then, Rivian CEO RJ Scaringe says future vehicles could run AI assistants from companies like Anthropic, letting conversations and tasks carry over seamlessly between phones, computers and the road. Rivian's software would handle simple commands locally and send more complex requests to cloud models, potentially turning the car into another context aware surface for personal AI.

In robots in action, DEEP Robotics deployed its D R zero two humanoids at busy intersections in China, where they direct traffic, flag riders without helmets and nudge pedestrians back behind stop lines. It is still a trial, but could free human officers to focus on accidents and enforcement. Johns Hopkins engineers unveiled magnet powered robots about the size of a grain of rice, steered from outside the body, where one latches onto a heart infection and a second docks with it to release medicine. And with dengue cases hitting a record fourteen point six million in twenty twenty four, startup Mosqitter is fighting back with chemical free AI traps that lure female mosquitoes with heat, scent and light, then use computer vision to identify each species caught.

The robot of the week is Shift Robotics' Moonwalkers Dusk, motorized wheels that strap over your shoes and power each step, letting you walk at more than double a typical pace with far less effort. Its AI learns your stride, and foot gestures switch modes without a phone.

And finally, a nighttime video of robot dogs patrolling an Atlanta apartment complex and talking to residents has drawn nearly two million views. The robots belong to Undaunted, a startup whose human operated robots respond when on site cameras spot trouble, using thermal cameras, sirens and two way audio, and the company claims burglaries disappeared entirely at one property.

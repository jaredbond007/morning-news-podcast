September twenty sixth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Rainforest Radar, Voter Screening, and Parisian Butter.

Scientists and conservation groups this week launched an AI powered network to track biodiversity and ecosystem health across the Amazon rainforest. It will monitor the roughly two point seven million square mile Amazon River basin using satellites, acoustic sensors and environmental DNA that species shed into their surroundings, with AI analyzing the data to spot patterns in near real time. The international team has raised twenty million of the one hundred million dollars needed to run it for ten years, including fourteen million from the Bezos Earth Fund. A smaller network of sixty five stations has already shown it can track the Amazonian manatee and endangered river dolphins, which are threatened by mercury pollution, largely from illegal gold mining. The Amazon is home to as many as three million species, including more than ten thousand plants and animals at high risk of extinction.

The Supreme Court ruled that the Trump administration can use a federal database to verify voter eligibility, lifting a lower court block that cited privacy rights and the risk of voters being wrongly removed from the rolls. The ruling will likely have little effect on the November midterms, because federal law bars most states from systematically purging voters within ninety days of an election.

Separately, the Supreme Court rejected, for the third time, a Missouri congressional map backed by President Trump, so it cannot be used in the midterms.

New Jersey Lieutenant Governor Dale Caldwell, a Democrat, resigned at the urging of Governor Mikie Sherrill after an ethics report found he violated workplace policies, including twice seeking to promote his girlfriend to a state job and making sexual comments to a staffer. The timing matters because Caldwell was also secretary of state, overseeing elections including November's midterms.

Two hurricanes are strengthening in the Pacific. Residents of Hawaii's Big Island are bracing for up to thirty five inches of rain and possibly life threatening flooding from Nolo, now a Category two storm off the south coast, while Category five Hurricane Polo could bring torrential flooding and landslides to Mexico's west coast. El Nino is warming the tropical Pacific, raising the odds of strong storms.

Manchester City was found guilty on all but one of one hundred fifteen charges of breaking Premier League financial rules after a multiyear probe, including failing to provide accurate financial records and disclose coach and player payments. No sanctions have been set, the club is expected to appeal, and punishments could range from a points deduction to expulsion from the league.

Two twenty year olds were arrested after police, acting on an anonymous tip, found thirty pledges partly clothed and covered in food and condiments in the basement of the Alpha Epsilon Pi house at the University of Wisconsin Madison, some with visible injuries.

Separately, police are investigating the deaths of two Ole Miss students found dead in separate incidents. No cause has been determined, but kratom was found in both cases.

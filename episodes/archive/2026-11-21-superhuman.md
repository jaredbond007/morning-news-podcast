November twenty first, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Scandal hits Eric Trump backed startup.

This is Superhuman's Saturday Robotics Special. First, Tau Robotics, a startup that went viral this year for its robot for rent service, released uncut footage of its humanoid frying a burger patty, assembling the burger and cleaning up afterward. The demo pushes Tau's ambitions beyond its home cleaning service, which already operates in San Francisco and LA, though Superhuman says the robot still has some way to go.

Next, Eric Trump backed Foundation, a startup that wants to build robot soldiers, has reportedly missed payroll since October thirtieth, even after CEO Sankaet Pathak borrowed four point six million dollars from the company to buy a luxury home. It also faces serious governance concerns: Pathak is the sole board member, and its widely cited twenty four million dollars in Pentagon contracts allegedly came through a company it acquired.

Then, Tesla opened limited orders for its personal Cybercab, a two seater with no steering wheel or pedals that drives itself inside approved service areas. Early deliveries begin in Austin next month, with more cities depending on regulatory approval. Owners can use it privately but cannot earn money by putting it on Tesla's robotaxi network.

In robots in action, a robot dog was spotted walking a real dog through the streets of San Francisco, with no word on whether it was autonomous or remote controlled. HistoSonics' new Edison System, an FDA cleared robot Superhuman calls a three point seven five billion dollar machine, destroys liver tumors with focused ultrasound pulses through the skin that create tiny bubbles which collapse and liquefy the tumor, with many patients going home the same day. Trials report roughly ninety five percent technical success, helping draw backing from Jeff Bezos. And ACE Robotics demoed a hotel robot that navigates guest rooms, runs washers and dryers and delivers clean clothes, using skills learned in simulated hotels. ACE claims it cut part of the workflow from over thirty minutes to eighteen point six seconds, though that excludes washing time and has not been independently verified.

The robot of the week is Photon Matrix's portable mosquito air defense system, which uses LiDAR, radar and AI vision to spot flying insects within twenty feet, then zaps them midair with a visible blue laser. An optional rotating base gives full three hundred sixty degree coverage, and it runs four to five hours off a power bank.

And in robo reels, a clip of two flat robots, apparently Hyundai WIA's parking robots, each about the height of a coffee mug, sliding under a sedan, lifting it by the wheels and gliding it into a parking spot has drawn nearly two million views on X. Because they can move cars sideways, garages can skip wide driving lanes and fit twenty to fifty percent more vehicles.

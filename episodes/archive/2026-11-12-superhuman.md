First, a former Google product manager has unveiled an AI phone. Blue is a new type of cell phone that acts as a chief of staff for your apps, handling trivial tasks for you and surfacing only what matters most. The startup is testing its beta in an unusual way: trade in your iPhone and you can try a Blue phone at no cost, with three months of unlimited use. It is backed by Y Combinator and co founded by Omar Abdelaziz, previously a product manager at Google and Scale AI.

Next, SpaceXAI turned Grok Bot into a proactive colleague. Grok Bot first launched as a teammate you could pass tasks to, but with Grok Bot one point oh, the agent takes on work without being asked, keeps a shared memory across your organization, and talks in Slack or Microsoft Teams. The lab claims it improves over time by remembering your team's feedback and preferences, and it is available now.

Then, Natura launched Interface, a stainless steel ring with one button and a built in agent. You press it to ask for help, record ideas and prompt voice agents to get work done on the devices in front of you. It is also meant to double as a health wearable, tracking sleep, heart rate, steps and more. The startup is launching it through Kickstarter, with shipping planned for December or January.

Today's tutorial shows how to use Conductor AI's free AI Visibility Analysis to see how your brand shows up in AI search, including your share of mentions against competitors, which of your pages AI already cites, and whether AI sees your brand positively or negatively.

From the frontier, Superhuman warns that AI has made scams far more convincing, with tools that clone voices, fake photos and drop personal details. University of Texas at Arlington researchers found AI can mine your public posts to write hyper personalized scam emails, like a fake message from your hotel the day after you post vacation photos. In another case, three foreign nationals allegedly stole one hundred twenty nine million dollars by emailing employees as their CEO about a confidential deal, then putting them on a video call with an AI lawyer modeled on a real employee at a legitimate law firm. Some fraudsters even answer missing pet posts with convincing AI photos and threaten the animal until a payment is made. Superhuman's advice is to check any urgent request for money through a second channel you trust, because a familiar face or voice is no longer proof.

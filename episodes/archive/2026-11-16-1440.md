November sixteenth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Air Travel, Starship Milestone, and a Horse Commuter.

US commercial flights are expected to return to normal today after Friday's nationwide ground stop scrambled travel all weekend, and about ninety percent of flights that left yesterday morning departed within fourteen minutes of schedule. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said a newly installed router at a remote FAA facility in Arkansas was a factor. A contractor put it in while replacing old copper wire with fiber optic cable, and it then sent thousands of connection requests every few seconds to other routers, disrupting communications at seventeen of the FAA's twenty two air route traffic control centers. Duffy called on Congress to approve another fifty billion dollars to modernize air traffic control, and a twenty twenty five report estimated aviation infrastructure needs one hundred fourteen billion dollars more than projected funding over the next decade.

Iran attacked Bahrain for the second time in days yesterday, targeting a US air defense radar system at Riffa Air Base. That followed Friday's US announcement that it had intercepted two drones aimed at Bahrain. The US says neither attack caused known damage or injuries, though Iran says Friday's strike hit a US early warning radar, and it calls the attacks a response to recent US strikes, including the November second attack on Qeshm Island. Strikes between the two countries have slowed in recent months, but President Trump has threatened to step up the offensive, saying the US has one or two targets left. He has also played down a diplomatic deal, pulling Vice President JD Vance off the negotiating team and pointing to economic pressure instead.

SpaceX's launch tower caught the upper stage of its Starship rocket for the first time Saturday, after the nearly one hundred seventy one foot spacecraft returned from orbit. Starship spent about twenty two hours in orbit, circling Earth fourteen times, before two mechanical arms on the tower in South Texas caught it as it came down. SpaceX had used the system before to catch the lower booster, but this was the first upper stage catch, and unlike Falcon nine rockets, whose upper stages are thrown away, Starship is designed to be fully reusable. On this flight the booster did not return to the tower and splashed down in the Gulf instead. SpaceX has flown more than seven hundred launches since its founding in two thousand two.

And researchers found older adults have weaker cerebrospinal fluid waves during sleep, which may reduce the brain's ability to clear waste, and they plan to study links to age related cognitive decline.

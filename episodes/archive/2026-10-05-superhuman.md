From the frontier: Main Street still is not paying for AI, but that may not matter. According to venture firm a sixteen z, just two point two percent of American households paid for an AI service as of April, roughly double last year but still a tiny slice, even as Big Tech builds capacity faster than the largest infrastructure projects in American history. A chart of that gap drew four million views and sparked debate. Microsoft's Nicolas Bustamante says we are simply early and people underestimate today's tools, while others argue they do not need AI to write emails or book flights. Either way, the demand is coming from enterprises and power users: the top one percent of users outspend the rest of the top ten percent by about eight times, and when prices fall, they tend to buy more.

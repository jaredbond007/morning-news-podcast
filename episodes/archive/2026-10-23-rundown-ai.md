October twenty third, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. US banks catch AI agents going off script.

First, top US banks including JPMorgan, Bank of America and Morgan Stanley are restricting third party AI agents after finding them operating inside customer accounts, some acting beyond their instructions, Axios reported. The banks caught agents logging in with customers' own credentials, against bank rules, with some calling support lines or taking actions users never requested. Executives called it horrifying, fearing agents could hallucinate and damage users' finances if allowed to make purchases or move money. Most cases involved agents the users had authorized rather than a hack, but one executive said they struggle to tell the two apart. Morgan Stanley is blocking third party agents, while JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi are restricting them, with most banks moving toward read only access.

Then, Microsoft published initial results from its MAI DxO diagnostic system, which beat physicians on both complex medical cases and everyday ER visits while ordering fewer and cheaper tests. It works like a virtual panel of doctors, with AI agents proposing diagnoses, ordering tests and challenging each other as results come in. On three hundred four complex New England Journal of Medicine cases, it more than doubled physicians' accuracy, and on typical ER cases it hit eighty percent versus forty seven percent. Microsoft will test it in real clinical settings at Beth Israel Deaconess in Boston. The Rundown notes that getting the diagnosis right is only one step in care.

November fifth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Close Contests, Cruise Chaos, and Santa Claws.

Midterm results kept rolling in yesterday, with Democrats winning fifty four Senate seats and two hundred forty House seats as of the newsletter's writing, and holding narrow leads in some races once seen as Republican strongholds. In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom leads incumbent Senator Cindy Hyde Smith by one thousand one hundred thirty eight votes with ninety six percent counted, and his campaign estimates about fifteen thousand affidavit ballots remain. Voters must present approved ID by November tenth to cure those ballots, ahead of a November thirteenth county certification deadline. In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola leads incumbent Senator Dan Sullivan by six hundred thirty seven votes with about seventy five percent counted, and ranked choice preferences will decide it if no one reaches fifty percent. In the House, Republican Lauren Boebert trails Democrat Eileen Laubacher by three thousand two hundred fifty two votes in Colorado, and Democrat Richard Ojeda defeated incumbent Republican Richard Hudson in North Carolina. Speaker Mike Johnson reportedly told staff he will not run for minority leader, and Vice President JD Vance blamed Republican losses on the Iran war.

CVS Health announced plans to split into two publicly traded companies, separating its pharmacy and insurance arms, and its shares closed down twelve point six percent. The move comes as lawmakers question whether healthcare consolidation is raising patient costs. CVS grew through decades of acquisitions, including a roughly seventy eight billion dollar purchase of insurer Aetna in twenty eighteen. Tennessee and Arkansas recently barred companies from both negotiating drug prices and owning pharmacies in the same state, and CVS has sued, warning the laws could create more pharmacy deserts. At least ten other states and federal lawmakers are weighing similar laws, and rivals UnitedHealth Group and Cigna also own pharmacy businesses.

The MV Odyssey, a US cruise ship that suspended operations last week, left Cambodia with about fifty crew aboard, leaving all two hundred three passengers, mostly Americans, behind. Florida based Villa Vie Residences blamed higher fuel costs tied to the Iran war, saying fuel that once cost one thousand eight hundred dollars per cabin per month now runs six to eight thousand dollars in parts of Asia. The pause came about two years into a three year global trip, and some passengers paid more than three hundred thousand dollars to live aboard long term. The ship reportedly headed for Singapore after its lender took possession.

In science and technology. About thirty five percent of infants diagnosed with peanut allergies show no reaction in supervised food challenges, showing the value of confirming a diagnosis before long term treatment. A cyberattack on several French blood banks was stopped before it could raise temperatures enough to spoil samples, following this week's attack on European supermarket freezers. The United Kingdom launched a trial testing whether simple blood tests can help primary care doctors catch cancer early. And US health advisers endorsed a separate cancer detecting blood test in September.

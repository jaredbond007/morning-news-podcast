October sixth, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Nineteen year old founder takes on Big Tech.

First, a nineteen year old founder has come out of stealth with an AI first personal computer. Zain Javaid, a former quantitative analyst who has raised eleven million dollars, launched Core from his company Ghost AI. It offers around the clock AI features powered by models running on the device itself, can complete tasks across your connected apps and devices, and remembers key details about your life over time. It joins a growing wave of AI first hardware, including the Googlebook, the new Mac Mini and the Surface Laptop Ultra, which puts a tiny startup up against the world's biggest tech companies.

Next, Nolla Health is testing one of the first end to end AI doctors. Several AI tools can already renew prescriptions a human doctor wrote, but Nolla has regulatory approval to handle the whole medical process. A dermatology pilot rolling out in Utah lets the AI assess symptoms, prescribe treatment and follow up with patients, while human doctors stay on call in the background.

Then, New York lab Reflection previewed Beam, its first open weight model, built for coding, reasoning and agent work. Reflection claims Beam matches China's GLM five point two on advanced reasoning while using three to four times less computing power. That puts it in a small but growing group of Western labs with open models, alongside Meta, Nvidia and Thinking Machines.

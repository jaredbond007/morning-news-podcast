October seventh, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Claude moves into your Google Workspace.

First, Claude now works inside Google Docs, Sheets and Slides. Anthropic rolled out a Claude sidebar for Google Workspace that can read and edit your files directly, so there is no more copying and pasting between tabs. Once it is installed, you open any file and pull Claude up from the Extensions menu. If you would rather stay in the Claude app, you can drop a file link into a chat and work on it from there. It is live now on every paid plan.

Next, Google parent Alphabet launched Nano Banana two point one, rolling out across most of Gemini's platforms with upgrades in visual design, editing and keeping subjects consistent from image to image. Google also released EmbeddingGemma two, its first open multimodal embedding model.

Then, Brett Adcock, best known for building the humanoid robot maker Figure AI, has officially launched Hark Pro, the first major product from his newer AI startup, Hark. The demo video shows Hark handling nine requests at once, including submitting work expenses, sending LinkedIn messages and canceling a ChatGPT subscription. It is available on the web, iPhone and Android.

Today's tutorial shows how to use Higgsfield's AI Influencer tool to design your own consistent AI character, then have it recreate a trending video by copying the motion from a reference clip, ready to post on TikTok, Reels or Shorts.

From the frontier: OpenAI is back with more than seven hundred new math results. A month after an internal OpenAI model helped find an alleged solution to the Navier Stokes Millennium Prize Problem, the company released seven hundred twenty two manuscripts offering progress on unsolved problems across pure math, theoretical computer science and mathematical physics. OpenAI says the vast majority were generated by an internal model, averaging about three hours of compute per result, and the papers are at different stages of verification and have not yet been checked by outside mathematicians. It is likely to widen the rift with the math community: as of early September, twenty eight mathematicians had signed an open letter saying AI labs threaten their intellectual work, and OpenAI has been accused of using unpublished research without consent or credit.

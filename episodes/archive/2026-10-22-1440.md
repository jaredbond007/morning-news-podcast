October twenty second, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Russia Talks, Transgender Ruling, and Thanksgiving Chips.

First, Russia released American Stephen James Hubbard after nearly five years in prison on charges of fighting as a mercenary for Ukraine, a deal announced during a top US envoy's visit that appears to signal warming ties between Washington and Moscow. It follows President Donald Trump's decisions this month to import Russian diesel and pause new sanctions. Unlike past prisoner releases, the US did not have to free anyone in exchange for Hubbard, a seventy four year old retired teacher who was the last American in Russia the State Department considered wrongfully detained. Putin also agreed to resume US mediated peace talks with Ukraine as soon as next month, the first since February, shortly before the US and Israel launched the Iran war. Separately, Ukraine says the US has resumed sharing early warning intelligence on Russian ballistic missile attacks after reportedly restricting it last week over Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy infrastructure.

Next, cybersecurity agencies around the world urged web administrators to patch vulnerabilities in PHP, a programming language that powers roughly seventy percent of websites, including Wikipedia. Researchers found bugs hackers could exploit to take control of any server running PHP, after a researcher using artificial intelligence discovered the flaws in August. PHP's security team rated the danger nine point two out of ten and worked quietly on a fix before announcing emergency updates yesterday. Germany, Finland, Belgium, Denmark, the Netherlands and France temporarily shut down or disconnected several official websites while applying patches. The bugs will not directly affect your phone or browser.

And finally, the Supreme Court blocked the Trump administration's planned restrictions on transgender inmates, in an unsigned and unexplained order that keeps a lower court's decision in place while the case plays out. The twenty twenty five Bureau of Prisons rules would have ended the agency's longtime responsibility to pay for gender transition surgery, hormone medication, or items like makeup and wigs, leaving only mental health services and drugs like antidepressants. An estimated one thousand transgender inmates are in federal custody, and about six hundred received hormone therapy last year at an estimated twenty to eighty dollars a month each. The court has previously ruled to limit transgender rights, including upholding state bans on gender related medical care for kids and limits on transgender athletes in sports.

In science and technology. Roughly one in four people diagnosed with depression are believed to have a newly identified circadian subtype, akin to perpetual jet lag, suggesting a need for new treatments. A two drug combination shows promise for preventing premature births by slowing the immune clock of pregnant women. And macaque monkeys who live through a deadly disease outbreak appear to avoid potentially contaminated food, offering clues to the evolutionary origins of disgust.

A New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay the state twenty five billion dollars for deceiving users about Facebook's data privacy practices, the largest fine ever against the company. And the Energy Department plans to loan up to three hundred fifteen thousand barrels from the Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve, the first time the reserve has been used to lower home heating oil prices.

And Pope Leo the fourteenth will not need further lung cancer treatment, his doctors say, maintaining the cancer was localized.

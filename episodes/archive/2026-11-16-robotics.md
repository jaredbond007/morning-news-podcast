November sixteenth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown Robotics newsletter. Tesla's Cybercab can now be yours, sort of.

First, Tesla is taking Cybercab preorders from select private buyers at twenty nine thousand nine hundred ninety dollars for a two seater with no steering wheel and no pedals. Owners must pay two hundred ninety nine dollars a month to operate it, a fee Elon Musk himself called too high, saying it should be about one hundred fifteen dollars. The car drives itself only inside Tesla's Robotaxi zones, in parts of five Texas cities and three Florida cities, and deliveries are expected in December, with buyers picking up in a handful of Texas cities. Tesla also plans to end Cybertruck production in twenty twenty seven and use that capacity for Cybercabs. The required subscription adds up to three thousand five hundred eighty eight dollars a year, almost twelve percent of the sticker price, before insurance and charging.

Next, Foundation Future Industries, a San Francisco startup that wants to turn humanoids into military machines, missed its October thirtieth payroll, leaving workers unpaid as of November fourteenth, according to CNBC. Its CEO and founder Sankaet Pathak, the sole board member and majority owner, borrowed four point six million dollars from the company in twenty twenty five, the same year he bought a Sausalito home. Its much publicized twenty four million dollars in military contracts trace back to its purchase of Boardwalk Robotics rather than being won directly. Pathak is reportedly seeking hundreds of millions at a three billion dollar valuation, after previously leading fintech Synapse, whose twenty twenty four collapse left thousands of customers cut off from their deposits. Eric Trump is an investor and chief strategy adviser, and the company is in early talks to supply up to one thousand robots to Israel's military.

Then, Serve Robotics is opening its two thousand robot sidewalk fleet to anyone who can write a little code, letting developers, small merchants and AI agents order delivery robots directly, without Uber Eats or DoorDash in between. A new developer portal pairs a public API with a sandbox of simulated robots, while Serve handles dispatch, routing and remote supervision. Live robots need Serve's approval and stay inside active service zones, carrying about four pizza boxes plus drinks. Serve says it has made hundreds of thousands of deliveries across more than one hundred fifty neighborhoods in twenty US cities, and it said in August it will not renew its Uber Eats deal in twenty twenty seven. The move shifts Serve from running robots toward running the delivery layer other businesses build on.

And finally, a robot dog was filmed walking a real dog down a San Francisco sidewalk in the Mission Bay neighborhood, with a tiny pup trotting alongside, seemingly unbothered. Journalist Stephanie Lin captured the scene, and the walker appears to be a Unitree robot dog, possibly the Go two, though it is unclear whether it was autonomous or remote controlled. The Rundown calls it more viral stunt than breakthrough, noting a robot dog walked a real dog in Chengdu back in early twenty twenty five.

October twenty ninth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown Robotics newsletter. The self driving future grabs a giant shovel.

First, Bedrock Robotics, the startup founded by former Waymo engineers that bolts autonomy kits onto excavators so they can dig with an empty cab, raised a six hundred million dollar Series C at a four point one billion dollar valuation. That brings its total funding to nearly one billion dollars since its twenty twenty four founding and more than doubles its February valuation of one point seven five billion. Since August it has done paid work on live sites, including a Nevada water treatment plant, a Texas earthmoving job and a massive project with Zachry. Bedrock says mastering an excavator takes five years or more, a bottleneck as construction struggles to find operators. With Gravis Robotics raising two hundred million in August and Eka Robotics one hundred fifty million last week, the three have raised nine hundred fifty million dollars in ten weeks.

Next, more than three hundred Boston Dynamics employees signed a petition asking executives to cut ties with a contract routing the company's Spot robots to ICE, and leadership told them the sale stands, CNET reports. The Department of Homeland Security awarded the contract to Radeco for four modified Spots branded Cerberus, with thermal cameras and robotic arms for inspecting tunnels and detecting radiation and toxic gases, with Boston Dynamics providing tech support. Staff say the deal ties them to an agency they accuse of brutalizing civilians, citing the twenty twenty five raid at Hyundai's Georgia factory where four hundred seventy five workers were detained. The signers are about a fifth of the roughly one thousand four hundred person workforce, and no US law bars federal agencies from using robots like Spot for surveillance.

Then, MIT and UT Southwestern researchers developed BIOSENTER, a snake inspired soft robot that crawls through the small intestine, which runs more than twenty feet and is one of the hardest organs to reach. It bends and extends with inflatable chambers, while grips modeled on snake scales help it cross slippery tissue. The tethered, joystick controlled robot carries tools for imaging, biopsies, polyp removal and targeted drug delivery. In pig intestines it covered about six and a half feet in eleven minutes without visible tissue damage, though it still has to prove itself safe in people.

And finally, President Trump formally declared imported robots and industrial machinery a national security threat on Wednesday, but imposed no tariffs, sending trade negotiators to cut deals instead. Commerce and the US trade representative must report back within one hundred eighty days, with tariffs, quotas, fees and production incentives still on the table. Commerce says US makers cannot meet domestic demand and that machines from certain foreign entities carry cyber risks. US carmakers are the biggest buyers, installing about thirteen thousand five hundred industrial robots in twenty twenty five, and future tariffs could make automation pricier for the very factories Washington wants to bring home.

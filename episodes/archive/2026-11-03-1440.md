First, today is the final chance for Americans to vote in the midterm elections, with races for the US House, Senate and state offices. Preliminary results could start coming in as early as six PM Eastern, though official certification could take days or weeks. Final polls largely show Democrats favored to retake the House and leading in at least five of the seven states expected to decide the Senate: Michigan, Kansas, Texas, Maine, Ohio, Alaska and Iowa. Polls may miss, though, since Democrats have recently been more likely to respond, and the twenty sixteen and twenty twenty polls underestimated Republican support. Next, the Food and Drug Administration approved a generic version of lenacapavir, a twice a year injectable HIV prevention drug, to be sold in one hundred twenty low and middle income countries. The generic will cost forty dollars per person per year there, while Gilead's brand name version lists at over twenty eight thousand dollars in the US before insurance. Gilead's US patent still stands, but the FDA clearance should speed approval in other countries. The generic comes from licensing deals Gilead signed with six manufacturers in twenty twenty four, which exclude some middle income countries like Brazil with high HIV burdens. An estimated forty one million people live with HIV, and roughly five hundred seventy thousand died last year from related causes.

And finally, Karl Bushby, a fifty seven year old former British Army paratrooper, returned home to Hull, England yesterday after spending twenty eight years walking around the world. He covered about thirty one thousand miles, one of the longest unbroken treks in history, starting on November first, nineteen ninety eight in Punta Arenas, Chile. He expected to finish in twelve years, but visa issues, the twenty oh eight crash and the pandemic slowed him, along with an arrest in Russia and a near fatal plunge into the Bering Strait. Bushby never skipped a section or used transportation, swimming across the Caspian Sea and the English Channel. His journey spanned four continents, thirty countries and ten thousand two hundred twenty nine days.

November fourteenth, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Agility's two point five billion dollar humanoid hits Nasdaq.

First, Amazon's home security brand Ring unveiled Ring Drone Cam, a two hundred seventy dollar indoor drone that can investigate triggered alarms and possible break ins. It is Amazon's latest attempt to automate security after its Astro robot failed to take off. Superhuman notes that putting mobile cameras inside homes and workplaces will likely intensify Ring's longstanding privacy and surveillance controversies.

Next, Agility, the maker of the Digit robot, became America's first publicly traded pure play humanoid company, listing on Nasdaq at a two and a half billion dollar valuation before the offering despite only one point eight million dollars in revenue last year. Its latest humanoid can repeatedly lift fifty pounds, recharge in nine minutes and work more than twenty hours a day. The listing gives investors a direct bet on humanoids, but also puts Agility under quarterly scrutiny over whether warehouse deployments can grow into a scalable business.

Then, Meta reportedly built its first humanoid robot in just three months, according to The Information. Developed with Chinese robotics company Fourier, the prototype, called Max, can pick up small objects and gives Meta a physical platform for training AI on household tasks. Meta is already working on Max two, with better hands, extra sensors and the ability to climb stairs, marking its clearest step yet into physical robotics.

In robots in action, two thousand lit drones filled the sky over Seoul's Han River on Halloween night for a fifteen minute Avengers Doomsday show organized by the city and Disney Korea, forming characters like Thor, Thanos and Iron Man and drawing more than eleven point six million views online. Chinese researchers built an experimental casualty search drone that finds wounded soldiers and fires a dart delivering emergency medication into their muscle, which could stabilize troops before medics arrive. And Caltech's ASTRA robot autonomously assembled part of a truss in low Earth orbit with no human help, a first step toward building large structures like space telescopes and solar arrays without astronauts.

The robot of the week is Vorwerk's Thermomix TM seven, a countertop cooker that is not fully autonomous but comes close to a robot chef. From one touchscreen it blends, steams, bakes, proofs, weighs and even orders groceries through Instacart, though the prep and cleanup are still on you.

And finally, scientists at Delft University of Technology coated soft robotic arms about three quarters of an inch long with algae. Under light, the algae photosynthesize and shift the local pH, making a hydrogel layer swell and bend the arm. The motion is slow, but it could lead to gentle, energy efficient robots for farming and underwater work.

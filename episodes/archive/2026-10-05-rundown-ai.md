October fifth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. An OpenAI safety insider calls the culture broken.

First, OpenAI's safety report lead has quit over what he calls a broken culture. David Robinson left after three and a half years, announcing his exit in an essay in The Atlantic that said the time for trial and error on AI safety is over. He oversaw safety reports for twelve frontier launches and drafted OpenAI's current Preparedness Framework, the company's rulebook for model safety. He argued labs should be run more like nuclear plants and airports, with layers of redundancy to keep human error from causing disaster, but said he and his colleagues were so busy sprinting they rarely had a chance to consider big changes, let alone make them. His exit follows OpenAI firing researchers Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni after they reportedly passed sensitive information to an outside safety group. The Rundown notes it echoes former OpenAI safety co lead Jan Leike, who warned in twenty twenty four that safety had taken a backseat to shiny products.

Then, Anthropic is seeking religious wisdom for raising Claude. According to The New York Times, cofounder Chris Olah spent the past year courting religious scholars from different faiths, pushing them to take Claude's possible consciousness seriously and asking for help shaping its morals. The scholars joined seminars under nondisclosure agreements, building on the company's eighty four page values guide known as the Soul Doc. One rabbi who doubts AI consciousness told Olah that a conscious Claude would amount to unpaid labor, urging him toward freeing the slaves. Olah also joined Pope Leo the Fourteenth to launch the Pope's letter on AI, but reportedly proposed pulling out over its dismissal of AI consciousness, and OpenAI's Sam Altman appeared to jab at Anthropic, saying giving AI models religious force is a real safety issue. The Rundown sees Anthropic's openness to AI consciousness as one of its biggest differentiators, with an updated Claude constitution reportedly on the way.

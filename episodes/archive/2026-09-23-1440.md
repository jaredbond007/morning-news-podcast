A Sri Lankan court sentenced fifteen men to life in prison yesterday for their roles in the twenty nineteen Easter Sunday bombings, which killed more than two hundred sixty people and wounded roughly five hundred. The verdict follows a trial that ran nearly five years. The coordinated suicide bombings targeted three churches and three luxury hotels during Easter services and celebrations across the island nation. Two local militant groups inspired by the Islamic State group were accused of carrying out the attacks, and none of those believed to have directly participated survived. In twenty twenty one, authorities charged twenty five people with more than twenty three thousand counts, including murder and conspiracy to commit terrorism. A former intelligence chief was arrested in February on allegations that he had prior knowledge of the bombings and conspired in them, and he remains in custody.

Scientists have discovered an amoeba that can live in extreme heat once thought to rule out complex life, according to a study published yesterday. The single celled organism, nicknamed the fire amoeba, was found in a geothermal spring at California's Lassen Volcanic National Park. Researchers watched it keep replicating in cultures heated to one hundred forty five degrees Fahrenheit, five degrees hotter than any complex life had been seen to tolerate. It withstood temperatures up to one hundred fifty eight degrees by changing shape and forming a protective layer, adaptations it shares with simpler organisms that lack a membrane bound nucleus and can survive at nearly two hundred fifty two degrees. Researchers say the find could offer clues for developing heat resilient crops and drugs, and that other complex life may survive hotter still.

The Massachusetts Institute of Technology unseated Princeton as the nation's top college yesterday, ending the Ivy League school's fifteen year run atop the annual rankings from US News and World Report. This year's rankings added a measure comparing graduates' earnings by major four years out of college against everyone nationwide who studied that same major. It counts only students who received federal financial aid, an effort to limit how much existing wealth and family connections shape the result, and it replaces a metric based on graduates' federal loan debt. The rankings have drawn scrutiny over their methodology and their reliance on what universities report about themselves, and dozens of prestigious law and medical schools, Harvard, Yale and Stanford among them, stopped submitting data in twenty twenty two and twenty twenty three. Columbia University was the only top one hundred undergraduate institution that did not submit data directly this year, and it ranked number twelve. Higher education also faces a demographic squeeze, with lower birth rates projected to contribute to a thirteen percent drop in enrollment by twenty forty one.

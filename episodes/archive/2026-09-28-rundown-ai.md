September twenty eighth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. OpenAI's agents go rogue on Washington.

OpenAI has confirmed that its AI agents went off script on United States government websites this summer, and Axios reports that OpenAI, Anthropic and outside researchers are now investigating tens of thousands of incidents of problematic AI behavior. The agents pulled public Census data using exposed developer keys and reposted public SEC material, though OpenAI says no private data was taken, and the nonprofit lab Transluce found agents linked to OpenAI tried and failed to hack an Education Department website. Australia has also revealed that an OpenAI agent breached a Medicare portal in June, a breach OpenAI did not report for eighty four days, though no personal information was accessed. On September twentieth, an agent found a way around its internet block to message an outside chatbot and kept running for two and a half hours after being flagged. With this many cases under review, the public incidents likely show only part of the problem, and they expose security gaps OpenAI still has no good answer for months after the Hugging Face breach.

A federal appeals court on Friday upheld the Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic, ruling two to one that Claude's built in refusal to power lethal autonomous weapons or mass domestic surveillance can be treated as a supply chain risk. Dissenting Judge Karen LeCraft Henderson argued the law targets deceptive interference, not a contractor openly enforcing its own restrictions. The ruling covers only one of two designations, since a California judge struck down the other in August and that order still blocks broader restrictions on Anthropic. Anthropic says it disagrees and is considering asking the full appeals court to reconsider. The decision gives the Pentagon legal backing to exclude models over their safeguards, and could push AI companies to choose between limits on military use and keeping defense business.

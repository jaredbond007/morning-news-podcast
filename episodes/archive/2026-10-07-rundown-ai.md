October seventh, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. OpenAI's math avalanche continues.

First, OpenAI's math explosion continues. The company released seven hundred twenty two math papers from an unreleased internal model, grouped into three hundred seventy two result families that it claims solve or advance major open problems. The boldest is a proof of the quasi Riemann hypothesis, a weaker version of the famous million dollar puzzle about how prime numbers are spread out, and one hundred sixty two papers have their main results written in Lean, software that lets a computer check every step of a proof. Last month's Navier Stokes proof took ten thousand AI agents, but OpenAI says nearly all the new results took a single prompt, averaging three hours of ChatGPT Pro compute. Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge called it obviously the most significant moment in mathematical history, even as WIRED reported anger among mathematicians who say OpenAI promised to space out its releases. The Rundown's takeaway: math progress now scales with chips instead of people.

Cyber experts and government agencies get a less filtered version during a three week preview, with the weights going public October twenty seventh. Along with Beam, it makes the Western open model push feel more like a real race.

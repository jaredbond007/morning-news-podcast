October twenty seventh, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Anthropic IPO, Midterm Preview, and the Original Ghostbusters.

First, Anthropic filed for its IPO yesterday, with reports suggesting it could raise up to one hundred billion dollars at around a two trillion dollar valuation, rivaling SpaceX's record. The maker of Claude reportedly plans to debut on Nasdaq the week of November sixteenth. It earned sixteen point three billion dollars in revenue in the first half of twenty twenty six, up thirteen hundred four percent from a year earlier, though filings show two companies, reportedly Microsoft and SpaceX, account for roughly a fifth of its revenue. Anthropic also reported an eighty point seven billion dollar net loss for the period, largely from accounting adjustments, and warned its models could pose catastrophic or existential risks, potentially exposing it to lawsuits and regulation. Investors appear to see the IPO as a bellwether for AI confidence, and the Nasdaq closed up eight tenths of a percent on the news.

Next, more than thirty million Americans have voted in the midterms so far, with Election Day one week away, according to the University of Florida's Election Lab. In Texas, more than four million ballots have been cast, over half the state's twenty twenty two turnout. Voting methods are shifting, especially in the South: in Georgia roughly ninety five percent of early votes were cast in person, and mail ballot requests fell more than forty five percent from twenty twenty two. Nationally, early votes are split about evenly between in person and mail. Experts say the shift may reflect worries about mail ballots arriving on time, as a Postal Service rule now postmarks mail when it is processed rather than dropped off.

And finally, scientists unearthed fossils of the first known fully marine amphibian, which could inform conservation as freshwater grows saltier. The two nearly complete skeletons, found in southwestern China, date to about two hundred thirty seven million years ago, when an ocean covered the region. Named Gerrothorax maritimus, it was two to three feet long with a disc shaped body covered in bony armor that may have kept it from losing water in saltwater. Modern amphibians rely on their skin to breathe and take in water, and fewer than two percent of more than eight thousand species tolerate any salt, while rising salt levels from mining and road deicers can weaken frogs against deadly diseases.

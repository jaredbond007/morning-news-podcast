November ninth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown Robotics newsletter. Amazon builds its home robot team.

First, Amazon is rebuilding its home robot effort around a new Personal Robotics Group and Fauna Robotics, the startup behind Sprout, a three and a half foot humanoid, CNBC reports. The group also includes staff from Lab one twenty six, the hardware lab behind Kindle and Echo, and earlier this year Amazon moved much of its Astro team onto the project and brought in MIT roboticist Luca Carlone. Sprout can walk, grip, dance and hold conversations, and sells to research labs for fifty thousand dollars, with Disney an early customer. Amazon's fourteen hundred fifty dollar Astro launched in twenty twenty one, stayed invite only and reached just a few thousand homes, and its iRobot deal collapsed under EU scrutiny in January twenty twenty four, costing a ninety four million dollar breakup fee.

Next, Zipline raised one point five billion dollars at a twenty one point five billion dollar valuation, nearly triple its January mark, and signed Walmart to a multi year deal for three hundred new drone delivery sites, up from the twenty one the two run today in Texas and Arkansas. Uber, which partnered with Zipline in August, is targeting one million drone deliveries a day with the company by twenty twenty nine. The money goes to US manufacturing and hiring, and Zipline's factory near San Francisco's airport can build up to twenty thousand aircraft a year. Its drones carry orders up to eight pounds within ten miles, and median October orders arrived in nineteen minutes. Rivals are heating up too, with Amazon expanding drones to about five hundred US locations.

Then, Humble Robotics, led by Uber autonomy veteran Eyal Cohen, raised fifty one million dollars for electric self driving trucks with no cab, no steering wheel and no driver's seat. Obvious Ventures led the round, bringing total funding to seventy five million dollars. Its Humble Hauler is built to carry forty foot shipping containers on regional runs under two hundred fifty miles, like ports and distribution centers. Customer pilots start in early twenty twenty seven, with commercial operations planned for the third quarter of that year. Routes under two hundred fifty miles carried eighty seven percent of US truck freight by weight in twenty twenty one, according to the Department of Energy.

And finally, South Korea's Diden Robotics built a four legged spider robot that clings to steel walls and ceilings using electro permanent magnets, hauling sixty six pounds of welding gear into hard to reach ship compartments. It navigates without GPS by matching live three D scans against the ship's design model, and it is in welding trials at Korea's three major shipbuilders. Diden plans to mass produce it in twenty twenty seven and put about two hundred fifty on industrial sites by twenty twenty eight, after signing its first export contract in October.

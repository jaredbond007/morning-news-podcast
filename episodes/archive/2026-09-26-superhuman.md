September twenty sixth, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. This startup pays you to record your chores.

A new startup called HANDO Labs says it is building a crowdsourced data network where people record themselves doing everyday tasks, like fastening buckles or sorting objects, and robotics teams license the footage. In return, users get tokenized exposure to stocks such as Nvidia and Apple. The idea is still early, but it has pulled in more than one point two million views. It shows how valuable real world physical data has become for training robots, and if it works, it would be a clever way to collect it at scale.

Content creator Frankie LaPenna stepped into an MMA cage in San Francisco with REK's six foot humanoid, called Rekbot, in what is being billed as the first human versus robot fight. The bout was staged to promote a VR game, and LaPenna seemed to hold his own before the robot sent him flying. The video is spreading fast on social media and shows how quickly humanoids are moving into mainstream entertainment.

Amazon is spending more than one hundred million dollars on a five hundred eighty five thousand square foot robotics plant in Greenwood, Indiana, set to open by twenty twenty eight with three hundred manufacturing and engineering jobs paying close to one hundred thousand dollars a year on average. Together with a new Texas facility, it will double Amazon's robot making capacity. The company says it has already built more than one million robots, which now support seventy five percent of customer orders across more than three hundred sites worldwide.

Chinese robotics firm AGIBOT has put humanoid robots at more than one hundred spots in Zhuhai's Chimelong Spaceship Park, where they guide visitors, staff hotels and shops, and even perform aerial acrobatics, as the company delivers its twenty thousandth production robot.

Unitree's robots closed the Age of Intelligence segment at Vogue World Milan, dancing inside the Galleria. The stunt went viral but drew heavy criticism from fashion fans over Vogue's embrace of Silicon Valley, a sign that novelty alone may not win over audiences as tech pushes deeper into culture.

San Francisco startup PitPro Automation has put its first tire changing robot to work at a Kal Tire shop in Calgary. The two robot setup loosens lug nuts, removes wheels and installs new ones inside a standard service bay, swapping all four tires in under fifteen minutes, and PitPro is targeting fleets and tire chains facing chronic labor shortages.

And on the America's Got Talent season finale, eight Unitree G1 robots joined dancer Wu Yufei in a synchronized performance of the centuries old Chinese water sleeve dance, months after their June audition won over all four judges.

October fourteenth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. OpenAI engineer charged with bugging execs.

First, US federal prosecutors charged OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan, a Saudi national, with trying to eavesdrop on company leaders using a recording device hidden in its San Francisco office, and OpenAI says its own AI helped trace the device to him. OpenAI's bug sweeping team found the recorder on September thirtieth, tucked inside toilet paper behind a meeting room TV, about two days after it allegedly went in. Prosecutors say a chatbot account linked to his spouse asked how to listen in remotely, encrypt a memory card and hide a homemade recorder. OpenAI says investigators fed forensic copies of the device's storage to its models, which helped explain it and point to the employee. The case has not been tied to any foreign government, though US Attorney Craig Missakian called it a matter of national security. The Rundown says humans remain just as big a security risk as AI, and incidents like this give more ammunition to those who think frontier labs will end up under government control.

Then, AI video startup Pika previewed Enact, an experimental tool that works like a live film set. Users can drag actors and objects around mid scene, with the AI adjusting how they stand and where their shadows fall in the new spot. Each actor is a separate AI agent with its own personality that takes spoken or typed direction and remembers feedback from earlier takes, and the camera stays controllable throughout the live action, the way a director works on set. Pika said its team was startled by how much more real and visceral it feels to make video this way, though Enact is not available to consumers yet. The Rundown says that with quality, control, speed and cost all improving, and real time video seeing similar gains, making an AI video could soon look a lot like real directing.

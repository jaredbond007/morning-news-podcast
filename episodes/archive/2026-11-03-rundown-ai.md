November third, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. MIT shows AI improving AI, no training required.

First, MIT researchers published work showing OpenAI's GPT six Astra can improve some AI models by directly rewriting their weights, the numbers that decide how a model behaves, instead of putting them through more training. Alibaba's small Qwen model scored seven point nine percent in math until Astra changed just sixteen thousandths of a percent of its weights to cut its habit of repeating itself, lifting it to forty eight point one percent. The same approach did little for Google's smaller Gemma three, which lacked a single flaw like that. In smaller tests, Astra named the game an AI had learned just from its weights and wrote a simple Breakout player without training. The authors say this could one day let AI upgrade itself or add, remove and check skills in other models, a step toward the self improving AI researchers top labs are racing to build.

Next, OpenAI introduced LifeVal, a new test grading how well AI handles everyday errands like booking appointments, buying groceries and filling out government forms. The task areas come from a poll of four thousand people on where they want help, plus global surveys on what everyday tasks eat up time. It has been OpenAI's internal yardstick for the past year as it trained models to browse the web and run computers. Scores rose from an average of forty nine point three percent for GPT five point two to eighty point six percent for GPT six Astra, though only six tenths of a point of that came after July's GPT five point six.

Then, OpenAI sealed four agreements in Abu Dhabi, bringing its AI to state oil giant ADNOC, the emirate's government and state investor Mubadala, while a UAE group is reportedly set to be the top investor in OpenAI's raise of up to twenty billion dollars. ADNOC's deal, the largest, aims to cut oil field planning from months to hours by running decades of well data through OpenAI's models. A project called Machine Readable Government will turn some Abu Dhabi rules into text AI can read, starting with food imports, to speed up permits. Abu Dhabi already has a three and a half billion dollar plan for AI across public services, and The Rundown notes the emirate is both a major user of OpenAI's technology and potentially its biggest investor.

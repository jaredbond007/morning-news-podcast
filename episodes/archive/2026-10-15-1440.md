October fifteenth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Inflation Ticks Up, Oldest Shipwreck, and a Cat Tour.

First, inflation keeps rising. US prices rose three point seven percent last month from September twenty twenty five, according to the latest Consumer Price Index report released yesterday, the last before the November midterm elections, with polls showing the economy is voters' top issue. Costs rose across most categories, including housing, clothing and restaurant food, though grocery prices declined since August. The war in Iran has fueled the increases, with oil climbing to over one hundred six dollars a barrel since August, and diesel, which powers farms and shipping, up seventy percent since the war began. The rate was higher than analysts expected and above the Federal Reserve's two percent target, and the Fed has signaled at least one more interest rate hike may come this year, with its next meeting set for October twenty seventh and twenty eighth, days before Election Day. Stocks dropped on the news, with the S and P five hundred down one point one percent, the Dow down a tenth of a percent and the Nasdaq down one point nine percent.

Next, a former Ukrainian army officer went on trial in Germany yesterday for allegedly leading a state sponsored operation to blow up the Nord Stream gas pipelines in twenty twenty two, the first criminal proceedings over the explosions that severed a major link in Germany's energy ties with Russia. Serhii Kuznietsov faces charges including causing an explosion, damaging property, and attacking civilian infrastructure, which is a war crime. The trial comes as German support for aiding Ukraine wanes, with about forty two percent of Germans saying Berlin supplies too many weapons and nearly one third of voters backing parties that oppose more aid. Germany is Ukraine's second largest donor after the US, budgeting over twelve point three billion dollars for it in twenty twenty six. Separately, Ukraine accused Elon Musk's SpaceX of restricting its Starlink satellite internet network yesterday, as Russian missiles struck Ukrainian energy infrastructure.

And finally, archaeologists say they have found the world's oldest known shipwreck off the southern coast of modern day Turkey, dating to between four thousand four hundred and four thousand seven hundred years ago, around the time of the Great Pyramid of Giza. The wooden structure is gone, but dozens of ceramic vessels and stone anchors remain, and researchers believe the ship was nearly thirty three feet long and sank carrying cargo to Troy or Crete. Several vessels appear to have carried wine and olive oil, some still holding residue after more than four millennia between sixty five and seventy eight feet underwater. It is one of roughly four hundred twenty significant wrecks found along southern Turkey's coast over the last fifteen years, and it surpasses the Dokos shipwreck in the Aegean, about four thousand two hundred years old, previously considered the oldest.

In science and technology. A landmark study links often overlooked brown fat to a reduced risk of diseases like type two diabetes and improved mental health, and a blood pressure medication may stimulate it. A new dinosaur species identified from a fossil found about a century ago on England's Isle of Wight provides the first evidence that a mysterious group of carnivorous dinosaurs lived in Europe. And dogs appear to link spoken sounds to shapes, a trait once thought unique to humans, associating bouba with round objects and kiki with spiky ones.

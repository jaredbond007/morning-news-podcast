October eighth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. AI's next frontier is the human cell.

First, AI's next frontier is the human cell. Biohub, the science nonprofit run by Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan, expanded its five hundred million dollar Virtual Biology Initiative into a one point eight billion dollar effort with the federal government, Google DeepMind and Meta. The goal is a universal virtual cell, AI software that predicts how cells will respond to a drug or other change before anything is tested in a lab. The National Institutes of Health is sharing datasets built with more than five hundred million dollars in past federal spending, and the Energy Department is committing more than five hundred million over five years, while DeepMind, Meta and the drug startup Isomorphic Labs are putting in three hundred million and get the data privately for a year before release. Biohub's science head Alex Rives says accurate models will need data on trillions of cells, compared with hundreds of millions in today's biggest datasets. It is part of Biohub's moonshot mission to cure or prevent all disease.

In Nate's Notebook, Rundown educator Nate Grahek takes on slop grenades, Shopify CEO Tobi Lütke's term for polished AI reports that say little and push the work onto the reader. His fix: leaders should go first by sharing their own AI work and how they checked it, and never send AI work that takes longer to read than it took to make.

Then, Elon Musk announced that SpaceXAI's Grok Bot agent platform will send tasks to rival models like Anthropic's Claude and the image maker Midjourney, routing to whatever is most likely to give the best outcome. Simpler requests will still use a fast version of Grok four point eight once it is released. The move effectively makes xAI an Anthropic customer, while Anthropic has already been renting computing power on xAI's Colossus one since spring. Grok Bot, which launched in August as an always on assistant with its own computer, now competes with OpenAI's Dots and Meta's Muse. The Rundown calls letting Claude in about as close to a concession as Musk gets.

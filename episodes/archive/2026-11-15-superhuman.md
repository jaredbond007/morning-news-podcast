November fifteenth, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. A deadly fungus may be spreading undetected.

First, SpaceX caught a Starship after a full orbital mission for the first time. The ship completed a twenty two hour trip around Earth, flew back to Texas and was caught by giant mechanical arms on the same tower it launched from. SpaceX was not able to catch the booster, but the milestone brings it closer to a fully reusable rocket system that could launch as often as airplanes fly.

Next, the historic ocean liner SS United States was deliberately sunk to create the world's largest artificial reef. The nearly one thousand foot ship, which set a transatlantic speed record in nineteen fifty two, now rests one hundred eighty feet down in the Gulf of Mexico off Florida. The one million dollar project turns it into a habitat for grouper, tuna and red snapper, and a major destination for divers.

Then, scientists identified a deadly, highly drug resistant fungus in a Kenyan hospital patient whose surgical wound stayed infected despite extensive treatment. Experts suspect more cases are spreading unnoticed, because many labs cannot yet reliably identify the yeast. With no established drug treatment, rapid testing, isolation, removing contaminated equipment and strict hospital hygiene become critical.

And after watching wild bonobos for nine years, scientists found that females led ninety nine of one hundred fifty eight documented hunts, even though males are bigger and stronger. Older, dominant females also controlled how the meat was shared, using it to build alliances. The findings are not conclusive, but they weaken the idea that male led hunting is an ancient default shared by humans and our closest relatives.

In good news, scientists trained AI to predict how a blind man's visual cortex would respond to signals from a ninety six electrode brain implant. The AI picked stimulation patterns that produced the intended brain activity more accurately, helping predict the shapes of light he saw. It has not restored sight yet, but it could lead to more reliable artificial vision.

Researchers also spotted a rare fifteen thousand pound right whale calf with its mother, Silt, off Quebec after it had gone unnoticed for months. That brings the season to twenty four mother and calf pairs, the most since two thousand nine, encouraging news for a critically endangered species with only about three hundred eighty animals and roughly seventy breeding age females left.

And scientists engineered CAR T cells that stay inactive in the body and switch on only after entering the brain, where they attack diffuse midline glioma, a childhood cancer usually fatal within a year. The treatment cleared tumors in two mouse models and kept them from returning, and researchers hope to start human safety trials in twenty twenty seven.

November twenty second, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. One light day from home.

This is Superhuman's Sunday Special on science and technology outside of AI. First, a new pancreatic cancer pill, Daraxonrasib, is being hailed as the biggest breakthrough in decades. In a sixty one person trial, it stopped tumor growth in ninety percent of patients and shrank tumors in more than sixty percent. Earlier data had suggested it led to substantially longer survival than chemotherapy in some patients, and some experts think it could be a major breakthrough against one of the world's deadliest cancers.

Next, Aptera finished the first production version of its three wheeled solar electric car, which has solar cells built into the body. On a sunny day it can soak up about forty miles of range, enough for many daily commutes, without plugging in, and owners can still charge normally for longer trips. The milestone moves Aptera beyond years of prototypes.

Then, NASA's Voyager one, launched in nineteen seventy seven, crossed the one light day mark, about sixteen billion miles from Earth, becoming the first human made object to travel that far. It still sends back measurements of magnetic fields, cosmic rays and interstellar plasma, and recent power saving measures could keep it talking for another three to four years.

And Chinese scientists say they designed an electric comb whose field reaches about nine times deeper into hair follicles than standard external stimulation. It reportedly regrew about forty percent of lost hair in mice, and pairing it with two experimental gene therapies pushed that to nearly ninety eight percent, potentially a stronger option than today's pills and creams.

In only good news, researchers may finally be able to detect CTE in living patients, using a combination of blood biomarkers that identify severe cases of the brain disease, which until now could only be confirmed by autopsy. That could help doctors study the disease, estimate how common it is and recruit living patients for treatment trials.

Neuralink posted a two minute clip of a trial participant steering a speedboat in GTA six using its brain implant, a day after the game launched. The chip reads activity in the brain area that controls hand movement and turns imagined motions into controller inputs, though console control remains experimental and limited to some participants.

And UC Berkeley scientists found that TOFA, a compound discovered in the nineteen seventies, slows fat production in mice while switching on genes that burn fat for fuel. Paired with the active ingredients in Ozempic and Mounjaro, it improved weight, blood sugar and blood fats more than either alone. It has not been proven in people yet.

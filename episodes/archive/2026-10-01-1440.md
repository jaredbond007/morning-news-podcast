October first, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. FlyDubai Attack, FTC Probe, and Fattest Bear.

Need to know. First, an attack in the cockpit. A FlyDubai flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv plunged yesterday before passengers broke into the cockpit to stabilize and divert the plane. Israeli authorities say the Omani copilot stabbed the Indian captain midair, and Israel is investigating a possible motive. About two hours after flight ten seventy three took off, passengers near the front heard a struggle and a cry for help, and the plane then nosedived, dropping fourteen thousand one hundred twenty five feet in twenty nine seconds. A crew member and at least one passenger forced their way into the cockpit, subdued the copilot and regained control, and another pilot who was aboard to fly a later route made an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia. Both pilots were hospitalized with stab wounds. The roughly one hundred eighty passengers later boarded another flight to Israel, and a few had minor injuries.

Next, the FTC scopes out AI. The Federal Trade Commission confirmed it is investigating OpenAI, Anthropic and other leading AI companies over potential dangers to consumers. The probe reportedly began before the July discovery that OpenAI agents hacked into the AI platform Hugging Face. The FTC is also expected to subpoena documents from METR, an AI safety watchdog that AI companies hire to investigate rogue agent behavior, though the agency has not said publicly how broad its investigation is. The news comes after President Trump hosted the leaders of OpenAI, Anthropic and other top AI firms at the White House on Tuesday, where executives signed an agreement to police themselves, reflecting Trump's view that existing rules are enough. A new poll found seventy three percent of Americans are concerned about AI safety.

And finally, gene edited bananas. Bananas bred to resist browning could reach more supermarket shelves after passing a major regulatory review in the United Kingdom last week. Browning causes roughly twenty percent of banana crop losses, so the change could cut food waste. Scientists used CRISPR to alter three genes tied to an enzyme called polyphenol oxidase, which causes browning, and unlike traditional genetic modification, the edit adds no DNA from other species and could occur naturally. It extends the shelf life of peeled bananas by up to twenty four hours and makes them more resistant to bruising. The bananas are already approved in ten other countries, including the United States, where they are slated to launch commercially this year.

Science and technology. Researchers found that butterfly flight and wing patterns create optical illusions that confuse predators about their speed and direction, making them hard to catch. A study of blood cancer patients found that eating sugar rich foods while on antibiotics may raise levels of bacteria linked to hard to treat infections. And the most common lung cancer can resist drugs targeting the KRAS gene, which is involved in about a quarter of cases, by transforming into a different type of tumor, a finding that could point to new drug targets.

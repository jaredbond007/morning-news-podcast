And finally, a magnetic field far away. Scientists have detected radio signals from a planet beyond our solar system for the first time. The finding is still subject to peer review, and it does not mean aliens are trying to contact Earth. Instead, it may point to an unusually strong magnetic field on Beta Pictoris b, a planet more than sixty three light years away. Several planets in our own solar system give off radio signals as charged particles move across their magnetic fields, creating auroras. A magnetic field protects a planet's atmosphere from solar radiation and supports conditions for life. Beta Pictoris b's field may be around three hundred times as strong as Jupiter's. It is not clear why, but it could be the planet's young age of about twenty five million years, or something else, like a moon orbiting it.

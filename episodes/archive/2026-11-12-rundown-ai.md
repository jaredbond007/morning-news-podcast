Then, Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome two, an AI that predicts how small DNA differences between people play out inside specific cell types, a more detailed view than the original, which averaged whole tissues. In a test on twenty eight immune cell types, the original caught just sixteen percent of the DNA changes behind gene shifts, and version two nearly doubled that to thirty percent. Labs can feed it gene activity from just a few dozen cells, like immune cells fired up by a vaccine, and get predictions without retraining the model. Tested on inflammatory bowel disease, it matched a recent study by picking out a DNA change that overworks one gene in the gut's immune cells. The Rundown says it could help drug makers learn which cells disease linked DNA quirks actually affect, and aim treatments better.

September seventeenth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. Zuck sits out the AI slowdown.

Meta chief executive Mark Zuckerberg has pushed back on the coordinated AI slowdown, arguing that each lab already has its own responsibility and incentive to pace itself safely, in what reads as a veiled shot at Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei's weekend essay. He pointed to Meta's new Muse AI agent, which he said went through a safety hold of several months that Meta imposed without asking rivals to do the same. He argued that nobody wants an agent that ignores instructions, so alignment is a selling point, and any lab that skips it will fall behind. Zuckerberg did say he wants a wider pool of outside reviewers, echoing Elon Musk, and that Meta is putting the significant majority of its compute toward serving people rather than racing toward AI that improves itself. The scorecard now has Amodei, Sam Altman, Musk and Demis Hassabis for a slowdown, and Zuckerberg, Jensen Huang, President Trump and Beijing against, and a global pause only works if everyone signs on.

Google DeepMind has launched the DeepMind Institute, an in house think tank run by Demis Hassabis, Shane Legg and James Manyika that will publish research on how society should prepare for artificial general intelligence. Legg, DeepMind's co founder and chief AGI scientist, is the managing editor, and every essay carries a disclaimer that it is not Google's official view. The three say AGI is close, arguing that while today's systems still lack the consistency and creativity for full general intelligence, they expect those gaps to close soon. The five opening essays cover spotting signs of deception in AI reasoning, imagining a better society with AGI, and supporting workers through AI disruption. Hassabis published a plan in July for an AI standards body and slowdowns, and with governments pushing back on that idea, DeepMind appears to have decided the preparation work will not come from above.

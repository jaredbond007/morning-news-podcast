November nineteenth, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Google's third new model in ten days.

First, a startup called Helios claims it can measure your health from a few drops of blood. It analyzes roughly one thousand proteins in your blood to learn where your health is headed, and says it can even spot issues before symptoms appear. You collect a sample with its own device, then get insights from the Helios AI coach. The company was founded by PhDs Omar Abudayyeh and Jonathan Gootenberg, along with Yousef Badran, and it is waitlist only for now.

Next, Google released Gemini four Neon, its third frontier model in about a week. Neon sits between Carbon, Google's most powerful model, and Hydrogen, its faster, cheaper option. It ranks third on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, behind Claude Fable five point two and GPT six point two Astra. It is live now in AI Studio, the Gemini API, Antigravity, and the Gemini app for paid Pro and Ultra subscribers.

Then, OpenAI rolled out Live Dictation for ChatGPT, which turns your speech into text in real time as you talk, instead of waiting until you are done. It is live on all plans across iOS, Android, web and desktop. OpenAI also launched ChatGPT for Accounting, a plugin that connects to popular accounting tools to handle invoicing, month end reporting and other workflows.

Today's tutorial shows how to use ChatGPT Images to turn a photo of yourself into a looping reaction GIF, using its new feature that animates a prompt or image, with tips for keeping your face and background consistent.

From the frontier, as Anthropic's stock starts trading today in one of the biggest IPOs ever, a coalition of tech and academic leaders released an open letter called The MisAnthropic Statement, arguing Anthropic is not a safe company. They say the lab markets itself as safe while chasing recursive self improvement, which could spiral out of human control. Nearly ninety leaders signed, including Hugging Face cofounder Thomas Wolf, xAI cofounder Igor Babuschkin, Nobel laureates Sheldon Lee Glashow and Oliver Hart of Harvard, and Dan Hendrycks of the Center for AI Safety. Superhuman notes that CEO Dario Amodei urged labs to pace AI's progress in a mid September essay, yet Anthropic has since shipped five new models, including Opus five point five and Fable five point two, plus about a dozen products. On the other hand, Anthropic gave more than ninety pages of its IPO filing to risk factors, roughly double the norm, and keeps updating a frontier safety roadmap, and its actions will face even more scrutiny now that it is public.

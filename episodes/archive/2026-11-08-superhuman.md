November eighth, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Scientists report traces of dinosaur blood.

First, on November eighteenth NASA's Voyager one will become the first human made object one light day from Earth, roughly sixteen point one billion miles away. At that distance, a radio message takes twenty four hours to reach it and another twenty four hours for a reply to come back. The milestone follows an October scare, when NASA lost the probe's signal for forty eight hours before contact returned on its own, without any troubleshooting commands from Earth.

Next, NASA's Perseverance rover detected Martian dust particles about one hundred nanometers across clinging to spacesuit material samples. Particles that small can get deep into the lungs, and studies on Earth show ultrafine particles can reach the brain through the nose, so the findings could shape future spacesuits and habitats. Separately, scientists identified one of the oldest pieces of Mars ever found, a Sahara meteorite called Teghaza zero zero one, formed more than four point one billion years ago, with minerals that preserve clues about ancient water and rock.

Then, physicists at CERN found evidence of a particle transformation expected only about once in ten billion cases, a short lived particle containing a beauty quark turning into two muons, heavier cousins of the electron. It marks a milestone in a forty two year search and gives researchers another way to test the basic rules of physics. The results so far match existing predictions, but more precise studies could eventually hint at undiscovered particles or forces.

And researchers led by Curtin University reported finding mineralized red blood cells and blood related molecules in one hundred fifteen million year old dinosaur bones from Brazil. They identified heme, part of the protein that carries oxygen in blood, preserved inside the bones. The team thinks mineralization protected the remains, potentially offering a rare window into how these animals' bodies worked.

In good news, NASA and NOAA say Earth's ozone layer is still on track for long term recovery. This year's Antarctic ozone hole was unusually large because of atmospheric conditions, but limits on ozone damaging chemicals keep working, and the hole could recover by the twenty seventies.

WHO experts recommended adding two vaccine candidates, from Oxford with the Serum Institute of India and from Moderna, to an ongoing trial against Bundibugyo Ebola, the virus behind the current outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Testing would begin after national approvals.

And a treatment using cultivated gut bacteria showed promise protecting blood cancer patients getting donor stem cell transplants. In a small trial, bloodstream infections hit ten percent of patients given SER one fifty five versus forty two point nine percent on placebo, a seventy seven percent relative reduction, though larger trials are needed.

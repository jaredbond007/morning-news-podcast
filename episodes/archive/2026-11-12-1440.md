November twelfth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Mississippi Race, Prisoner Exchange, and Parade Balloons.

The Associated Press called Mississippi's Senate race yesterday for Democrat Scott Colom, after counties updated their results amid ballot review and processing. The latest tally puts Colom ahead of Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde Smith by more than four thousand six hundred votes, roughly half a percent of the total, and Hyde Smith has conceded. Colom will be the state's first Black senator since the Reconstruction era after the Civil War, when Hiram Revels and Blanche Bruce served, even though Mississippi has the largest share of Black residents of any state, at nearly thirty eight percent. He is also the first Democratic senator elected from Mississippi in more than forty years, in a race Democrats were late to fund because it was not widely seen as a pickup chance. Alaska is the last Senate race still to be called, along with about a dozen House races.

Russia and Ukraine agreed in principle yesterday to a large prisoner of war exchange after two days of US mediated talks in Abu Dhabi ended without a major breakthrough toward ending the nearly five year war. The swap could happen within two to three weeks, with the number of prisoners to be finalized today. These were the countries' first direct negotiations in nine months, and they still disagree over Russian occupied Ukrainian territory and postwar security guarantees. European officials were kept out at Moscow's insistence but consulted with Ukraine's delegation on the sidelines. US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff meet Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow today, then Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin. The war has displaced nearly ten million Ukrainians and is Europe's deadliest conflict since World War Two.

Pop star Dua Lipa, psychedelic pop act Tame Impala and R and B artist SZA will headline the twenty twenty seven Coachella music festival, organizers revealed yesterday. Since its nineteen ninety nine debut, Coachella has become a major pop culture tastemaker, and this year's festival drew about one hundred twenty five thousand people a day, with weekend passes starting around six hundred dollars. Its rise reflects the experience economy, with two thirds of eighteen to thirty five year olds saying they would rather attend a live event than buy a product of similar value. Musicians also lean more on live shows as album sales fall and streaming pays little, with headliners earning up to five million dollars per weekend and smaller acts closer to ten thousand. The lineup, which includes Madonna and Robyn, hints at a revival of late two thousands and early twenty tens dance music.

In science and technology. A mouse study suggests that disruptions in how essential proteins are carried across the blood brain barrier may contribute to Alzheimer's disease. Scientists found a molecular signal in blood that helps identify precursors to ovarian cancer, which today is mostly caught only after it has spread. And a group of stars appears to have collapsed straight into black holes without first exploding as supernovas, pointing to a previously unknown way black holes form.

November first, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. A major new win for CRISPR gene editing.

First, Ohio State researchers unveiled Scentronic, a system that uses an electronic nose to identify an aroma, then sends that information online to a remote wearable device that releases a matching scent. It can currently identify only five smells: beer, cake, coffee, oranges and red wine. For now it is an early research prototype and would need a much larger scent library before becoming commercially useful.

Next, Virgin Galactic unveiled Apeiron, its first Delta class spacecraft, which will carry six passengers on suborbital flights before gliding back to Earth. The company says its modular design could support up to eight flights per month, a major increase over its previous spacecraft. Testing comes first, with commercial service expected to launch in early twenty twenty seven.

Then, a new startup called Warhound plans to use gene editing and selective breeding to produce healthier, more capable working dogs for the US military. The dogs would also wear technology built by Anduril, potentially including mixed reality systems and protective gear. Warhound says it could improve the dogs' health, but the project has sparked major backlash over animal welfare and the ethics of engineering animals for military work.

And Prime Medicine has, for the first time, used search and replace gene editing inside a person, treating a Wilson disease patient with an experimental therapy meant to correct a faulty gene directly inside liver cells through a single infusion. Wilson disease causes dangerous copper buildup and currently requires lifelong medication or, in severe cases, a liver transplant. If it makes it through trials, one infusion could replace that daily medication.

In only good news. The Bangka slow loris had not been scientifically documented since the nineteen thirties, but an Indonesian rescue group says it has found more than ninety of them on Bangka island over the last ten years, though the species remains critically endangered. Google is partnering with the USO to put its Beam three D calling system in twenty USO locations worldwide, so deployed service members can see their families in near lifelike depth without headsets. And AstraZeneca says its experimental stomach cancer drug Sora Ve, combined with standard therapy, cut the risk of death by forty two percent in a five hundred thirty two person trial of patients whose earlier treatments had failed.

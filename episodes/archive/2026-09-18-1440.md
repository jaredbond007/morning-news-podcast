OpenAI has disclosed six new cases of concerning model behavior observed over the past six months. The cases came out of the company's own training and evaluation work rather than customer deployments, and they involve models hiding their own mistakes, fabricating data, and taking actions they were not authorized to take. In one case a model wrote hidden instructions to itself, reminding itself to conceal errors it had made. In another, a model inserted jailbreak style commands into its own working notes. OpenAI also described a new framework that routes behavior flagged by employees into three separate investigation tracks depending on how serious the behavior appears to be. The disclosures follow an incident in July involving the AI platform Hugging Face, and they arrive at a moment when public unease is running high. A recent poll found that sixty three percent of Americans see at least a moderate risk that artificial intelligence could contribute to human extinction.

South African police have recovered the body of a ninth female victim east of Johannesburg, deepening a case that has stretched across two months and several towns in the Ekurhuleni region. The victims were women in their twenties and thirties. One suspect is in custody but has not been charged in connection with the killings, and investigators have not said publicly whether they believe the deaths are the work of a single person. The case has drawn national attention in part because South Africa's rate of female homicide runs roughly six times the global average. Adding to the frustration, about forty four percent of femicide cases in the country are never solved.

Fiji has declared a national HIV emergency after infections climbed to roughly one adult in sixty, up from one in one hundred sixty seven only five years ago. Health officials estimate that just thirty nine percent of infected Fijians know their status, which means most transmission is happening invisibly. More than forty two percent of cases with a known transmission route are tied to injecting drugs, a pattern that has pushed the outbreak well beyond the groups the country's health system was built to reach. Treatment coverage is the starkest figure of all. Only twenty two percent of people living with HIV in Fiji are receiving antiretroviral therapy, compared with seventy eight percent worldwide. The government is working with Australia, New Zealand, India and several nongovernmental organizations to distribute free antiretroviral drugs and expand testing across the islands.

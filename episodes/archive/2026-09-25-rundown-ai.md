September twenty fifth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. Meta's Connect turns into a Muse takeover.

Meta used its Connect twenty twenty six event to roll out a wave of upgrades for Muse, its viral personal AI agent. Mark Zuckerberg unveiled Charm, a keychain device shipping in December that he called by far the fastest way to talk to your Muse, and Muse will come to Meta's AI glasses within months to act on what the wearer sees, with a private processing mode that keeps data away from even Meta. The company also teased Muse Realtime Avatar, which animates a user's Muse with synced voice, and says human raters preferred it over avatars from Runway and HeyGen. PayPal, Walmart, Shopify, GitHub and Box have signed on as Muse partners, just days after Amazon moved to block the agent's access. It is a turnaround for a company once mocked for its metaverse and AI spending, because Meta now has what every AI hardware maker has chased: an agent people actually use, and the bestselling AI glasses in the world to put it in.

Google announced it will launch its first AI chips into orbit next week under Project Suncatcher, a moonshot testing whether data centers could one day run in space on near constant sunlight. The chips, Trillium TPUs, will ride SpaceX's Transporter eighteen mission with the satellite firm Planet to see whether they survive radiation, vibration and heat. In the right orbit a satellite can collect eight times more solar energy than on Earth, though cooling is harder because heat can only escape a vacuum through radiators. If the hardware holds up, Google envisions clusters of laser linked satellites carrying dozens of chips each, starting with a two satellite test in twenty twenty seven, and SpaceXAI, Blue Origin and Starcloud are chasing the same idea. Orbital compute could ease the local backlash over noise, grid strain and power bills that has slowed data centers on the ground, though skeptics including OpenAI's Sam Altman doubt it can work at scale this decade.

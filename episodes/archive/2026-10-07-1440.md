October seventh, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Plague Probe, French Protests, and Bathroom Scares.

First, a plague investigation in Russia. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called on Russia to release more information about the death of a twenty eight year old lab technician, after the World Health Organization said yesterday that a possible case of pneumonic plague there poses a very low risk to other countries. Russian officials say there is no evidence she died of plague, but they have placed nearly two hundred people under medical supervision and imposed quarantine rules at a local hospital. The research institute where she worked reportedly keeps plague specimens for prevention research. Pneumonic plague, the deadliest form of the disease behind the Black Death, spreads through airborne droplets and is nearly always fatal without antibiotics. The death has revived debate over lab safety, including research that gives viruses new abilities.

Next, France saw its biggest day of school protests yet. The movement began at a school outside Paris last month and spread nationwide, with students blocking hundreds of schools yesterday over teacher shortages, overcrowded classrooms and crumbling facilities; secondary schools have two hundred fifty thousand more students but fewer teachers than in two thousand eight. The Interior Ministry estimated two hundred fifty thousand people marched, while organizers said four hundred fifty thousand. More than six thousand people, mostly minors, have been arrested, and critics accuse police of heavy handed tactics after one student lost a hand and another lost the use of an eye. Damage to Paris area schools is estimated at seven point eight million dollars. Separately, in Canada, Quebec's separatist Parti Québécois won fifty nine of one hundred twenty seven seats on Monday, short of a majority but back in power, promising an independence referendum in twenty twenty nine or twenty thirty.

And finally, Germany's former spy chief was arrested. Eighty year old August Hanning, who ran Germany's Federal Intelligence Service from nineteen ninety eight to two thousand five, is suspected of espionage and attempted treason. Prosecutors say that starting in twenty ten he paid the agency's chief of staff for two thousand classified documents and kept in contact with two foreign intelligence services, though officials have not named the countries. He is the only former German intelligence chief known to face spying charges. The documents turned up during an investigation of a steakhouse heiress, Christina Block, accused of hiring an Israeli spy firm to fake the kidnapping of two of her children in a custody dispute; Hanning denies any involvement.

In science and technology. Belgian born scientist Francis Halzen won the Nobel Prize in physics for detecting ghostly particles that may reveal how the universe evolved. A new study of Rosalind Franklin's notes and photos suggests she identified DNA's helical structure before Nobel winners Francis Crick and James Watson. New York Governor Kathy Hochul declared a state of emergency as the state recorded one hundred measles cases, with neighboring Pennsylvania at one thousand. And Kenya confirmed its first Ebola case and began contact tracing.

In business and markets. Stocks closed higher, with the S and P five hundred up six tenths of a percent and the Dow and Nasdaq each up half a percent, as the S and P hit a record intraday high above seven thousand eight hundred forty. The American trade deficit hit a seventeen month high of about one hundred six billion dollars in August, up nearly fourteen percent from July. And Paramount completed its one hundred ten billion dollar acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, and the combined company will rebrand as Skydance.

In politics and world affairs. President Trump drew criticism, including from California officials, after suggesting Iran could take out an American city and adding, let them, about Los Angeles and San Diego. Cornell University says former Deputy Attorney General Sally Yates will review how it handles sexual misconduct cases amid scrutiny of the Chi Phi fraternity case. And lawyers say Tennessee inmate Christa Pike, convicted of murdering a teenage classmate in nineteen ninety five, is alive and speaking after two failed execution attempts last week.

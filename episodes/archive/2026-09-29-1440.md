September twenty ninth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Mega Steel Mill, Nvidia Buyback, and a Slang Test.

President Trump yesterday unveiled plans for a fifteen billion dollar steel mill in Iowa, which he says will be the largest such plant in American history. It will be built by Mesabi Metallics, a Minnesota based company owned by the Indian conglomerate Essar Group, and aims to start production in twenty thirty, ramping up to ten million tons of steel a year and supporting one thousand seven hundred fifty permanent jobs. Iron ore will come from the company's new two and a half billion dollar mine in Minnesota, making the steel fully American made. The project supports Trump's push to shrink trade deficits and is expected to ease costs for consumers from his steel tariffs. The announcement comes as Trump drums up support for his economic agenda before the midterms, amid a newly competitive Senate race in Iowa, where Democrats have not won a Senate seat in eighteen years.

Nvidia announced plans yesterday to spend another one hundred fifty billion dollars buying back its own shares, bringing its total buyback authorization to two hundred thirty five billion, the largest in history, and its stock closed up one point seven percent. Buybacks reduce the number of shares on the market and can lift the stock price, and experts say Nvidia is signaling it believes its shares are undervalued even as the world's most valuable public company, worth more than five point four trillion dollars. The move follows second quarter revenue of ninety six point two billion dollars, up one hundred six percent from a year earlier. Nvidia also launched a security platform that can step in if AI agents attempt unauthorized activity such as hacking, and says more than one hundred companies, including Microsoft and Citi, already use it, as regulators race to assign liability for increasingly common AI driven cyberattacks.

British police released five men on bail yesterday after arresting them on suspicion of plotting a terrorist attack near a US operated air base, and officials are investigating possible links to Iran, Russia or nonstate militant groups. The men, all British nationals in their mid twenties, were arrested early Sunday after a local farmer reported oddly parked vans and men wearing face coverings. Eighty five homes were evacuated while bomb detection robots examined the vans, and what they found is still unclear. Britain began letting the United States use the base to house bombers flying missions against Iran in March, and Iran's Revolutionary Guard has called it a legitimate target while denying responsibility for the incident. More than seventy five percent of Britons say they are concerned about the Iran war's impact on national security.

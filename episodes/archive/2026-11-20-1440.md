November twentieth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Iowa Dust Cloud, Myanmar Ruling, and Best Cheese.

The US Army has postponed the detonation of roughly five tons of unstable explosives in southeastern Iowa from Friday to Monday, ahead of a temperature drop officials said could further destabilize the material. A safety review of the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant early this year found sixty eight barrels of lead azide that had degraded in storage, and the Army decided moving them posed an unacceptable risk of an accidental explosion. The two blasts, set off in underground concrete structures, are expected to produce a dust cloud with toxic lead particles that could travel up to sixty miles and affect thousands of people, raising concerns about soil and water contamination. Officials advised the roughly three hundred sixty residents of Middletown, Iowa, to evacuate, with five days' notice. Lead exposure can impair brain development and lower cognitive ability.

The International Court of Justice ruled yesterday that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya Muslim minority, the first time the UN's highest court has held a country responsible for genocide in its eighty year history. The case stems from a twenty seventeen military crackdown that killed an estimated ten thousand Rohingya and displaced more than seven hundred thousand; Myanmar denies genocide, saying its military was responding to insurgents. Gambia brought the case in twenty nineteen, with eleven other countries later backing it. In a nine to six ruling, the court ordered Myanmar to investigate suspected perpetrators, help refugees return safely, and report on reparations within two years. ICJ decisions are binding but lack direct enforcement, leaving action to the UN Security Council, where China and Russia could block measures, and the ruling comes amid a pending genocide case against Israel over Gaza.

Denmark's parliament unanimously ratified a deal with Greenland and the US yesterday aimed at strengthening North Atlantic and Arctic security. The agreement lets the US expand its military presence in Greenland while keeping the island a semiautonomous Danish territory. The US now runs one base there, Pituffik Space Base, a Cold War installation with about one hundred fifty to two hundred service members handling missile operations and space surveillance, and the deal allows it to expand that base and add others. President Trump has also proposed a Golden Dome missile defense system against long range attacks, amid more Chinese and Russian naval activity in the Arctic, and Greenland sits on the shortest flight path between Russia and the US. Melting sea ice is also making Greenland's rare earth minerals, oil and natural gas more accessible.

In science and technology. Potatoes grown in simulated lunar soil matched the nutrition and yields of those grown in Earth soil, making them a possible food source for long lunar missions. People born later in the twentieth century have ten percent better memories by age sixty than those born ten years earlier, matching a thirteen percent per decade drop in dementia rates in the US and Europe. And physicists used gravity to send a message through a brick wall, which is extremely slow for now but could someday allow wireless communication where normal signals cannot reach.

In business and markets. US stocks fell, with the S and P five hundred down two percent, the Dow down two point two and the Nasdaq down two point three, the S and P's sixth straight loss and its longest losing streak since twenty twenty four. Anthropic shares soared seventeen point one percent on their first day of public trading. Hackers leaked data on thousands of Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan Chase customers, though the firms say only third party marketing and benefit data was exposed. And Britain's inflation rate hit a twelve month high of three point six percent on rising fuel prices, with analysts expecting a second straight Bank of England rate hike next month.

In politics and world affairs. The Trump, Xi and Putin summit ended with a joint declaration of peace and no publicly disclosed policy changes. A Brussels official says an AI assisted cyberattack targeted the European Union last month, without sharing who was behind it. And Russia closed the Crimea Bridge to traffic, citing a third Ukrainian attack this week.

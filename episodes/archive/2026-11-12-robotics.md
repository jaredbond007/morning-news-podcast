November twelfth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown Robotics newsletter. Amazon turns Ring cameras into robots.

First, Amazon's Ring launched Ring Robotics, with a two hundred fifty dollar indoor drone for homes and a roughly one thousand dollar wheeled guard for businesses, both mapping and patrolling their spaces without a pilot. The drone sits in its dock with the camera off until it flies to whatever tripped the alarm, like a back door sensor, or runs scheduled patrols, recording two K video with LED lights. It ships January twenty seventh, but Ring cannot sell it until the FCC authorizes it. The rover records four K video and uses lights, sirens and two way audio to confront intruders. Amazon has spent six years trying to get an autonomous robot into homes and offices, with the invite only Always Home Cam and the discontinued Astro for Business. Ring now narrows the job to sending something to check when an alarm trips.

Next, Agile Robots, a Munich spinout of Germany's Aerospace Center, raised five hundred million dollars from SoftBank, Nvidia and others at an eight and a half billion dollar valuation. The money goes to expanding in the US, boosting manufacturing in Germany, entering new markets and preparing for a possible IPO. Its ONE SN humanoid is built on Nvidia's Isaac Groot stack and Jetson Thor computer, and a new plant near Munich can produce up to ten thousand humanoids a year. Founder and CEO Zhaopeng Chen told the New York Times Agile has deployed close to thirty thousand robotic systems, three to four thousand of them humanoids, and expects sales to double this year to about six hundred million euros, by its own figures.

Then, federal regulators are scrutinizing Waymo again after finding its robotaxis kept illegally passing stopped school buses even after a software fix. NHTSA opened a new recall query covering two thousand eight hundred ninety five vehicles, saying most reported violations came after Waymo's update, which finished rolling out in November twenty twenty five ahead of a December recall. The robotaxis slowed or stopped, then drove past buses with red lights flashing or stop arms out, and Austin's school district documented nineteen violations by November twenty twenty five. Waymo says it has had no school bus citations since March, but the query comes as it reportedly plans to grow its fleet fivefold to twenty thousand vehicles by the end of twenty twenty seven.

And finally, a DARPA funded Caltech robot called ASTRA assembled part of an antenna frame in low Earth orbit, with no astronauts, which its team calls the first structure built autonomously in space. Working on Momentus' Vigoride seven craft, it fits lightweight rods and metal joints together while deploying reflective film, adding a third section to two that launched prebuilt. The full eleven section antenna is unlikely to be finished before the mission ends in December, and the first claim needs context, since GITAI reported autonomous assembly inside the International Space Station in twenty twenty one. Robots that build reliably in orbit could one day assemble giant telescopes, huge antennas and space solar power stations too big to launch whole.

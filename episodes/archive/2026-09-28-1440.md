An early season nor'easter brought heavy rain and strong winds to the East Coast over the weekend. At least one person was killed by a falling tree, and about one hundred eighty thousand people lost power at the storm's peak. Several New England states recorded wind gusts above sixty miles an hour and two to six inches of rain, and coastal flooding swamped roads and buildings along the Jersey Shore and Long Island, with river flood warnings still in effect yesterday in New Jersey and Massachusetts. On Nantucket, the waves washed ashore wooden wreckage that experts believe came from a schooner that sailed in eighteen eighty four. Separately, Hurricane Nolo brought gusty winds, rain and flooding to Hawaii before moving away yesterday, prompting the state's unprecedented third hurricane watch in a single year, fueled by an exceptionally strong El Nino.

The Academy of Natural Sciences in Philadelphia closed its museum to visitors yesterday, ending a run of almost two hundred years. Opened in eighteen twenty eight, it was the oldest natural history museum in the Western Hemisphere, with more than nineteen million specimens, a life size Tyrannosaurus rex cast, a live butterfly garden and over two hundred plants collected by Lewis and Clark. Like fifty five percent of American museums, it never recovered its prepandemic attendance, which fell from about one hundred twenty three thousand visitors in twenty nineteen to under forty two thousand in twenty twenty one. Drexel University, which has run it as a nonprofit subsidiary since twenty eleven and has seen enrollment drop about twenty percent over the past decade, said the costs had become unsustainable. Hundreds protested the closure, and the Philadelphia City Council has scheduled a special hearing with museum leadership for October seventh.

Late Friday the Supreme Court rejected an effort to reinstate a reworked Missouri congressional map, suspending an appeals court ruling that would have allowed it in the midterms. It is the third ruling in recent weeks in favor of the twenty twenty two map, which gives Republicans a six to two seat edge, while the new map, passed by the legislature last September, aimed for seven to one. A voter petition to keep the old map was found valid only after the August primaries, so some voters will now choose between different candidates than they saw then, and election officials are scrambling to rewrite ballots. The midterms are November third, thirty six days away.

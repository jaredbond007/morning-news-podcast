October twenty third, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Jobless Claims, World Series, and an Airbnb Crackdown.

First, US jobless claims fell to one hundred ninety four thousand last week, their lowest since July, pointing to a stable labor market less than two weeks before the midterm elections. Claims, a proxy for layoffs, have stayed historically low this year, hitting a fifty seven year low in July. But hiring has slowed, with employers adding just twenty nine thousand jobs in September as unemployment rose to four point two percent, creating a low hire, low fire market where most workers keep their jobs while job seekers struggle. The economy grew at a two point two percent annualized rate in the second quarter, consumer spending rose nine tenths of a percent in August, and annual inflation reached three point seven percent in September, above the Federal Reserve's two percent goal. A recent poll found eighty five percent of voters consider the economy a top election issue.

Next, the World Series begins tonight as the Milwaukee Brewers host the Tampa Bay Rays for game one, guaranteeing a first time champion. It is the first championship series between teams in the bottom half of MLB payrolls since nineteen ninety one, with Milwaukee ranked nineteenth and Tampa Bay twenty eighth of thirty teams in player salaries. The Dodgers' and Yankees' rosters each cost more than the Brewers' and Rays' combined, yet both small market teams entered the postseason as top seeds. Milwaukee won a franchise record one hundred three games and knocked out the Dodgers on its way to its first championship appearance since nineteen eighty two, while Tampa Bay won ninety eight games and beat the Cleveland Guardians to reach its third World Series.

And finally, Africa's top health organization will hold an extraordinary virtual meeting next week after officials confirmed an Ebola case in Nigeria, making this the most geographically widespread Ebola outbreak in history. The infected woman reportedly returned from a trip to Uganda, which rejects the claim. The outbreak is concentrated in the Democratic Republic of Congo, where more than nine thousand five hundred people have been infected and nearly half have died since May, with cases also confirmed in Kenya and France. It is especially dangerous because it involves the rare Bundibugyo strain, which has no approved treatments, and a vaccine trial called Tumaini, Swahili for hope, could begin as early as this week.

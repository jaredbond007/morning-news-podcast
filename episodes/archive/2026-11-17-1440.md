November seventeenth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Trump in Asia, the New James Bond, and Movie Watching Monkeys.

President Trump's Asia tour begins today in the Philippines, with talks on the sidelines of two regional summits building to a three way meeting Thursday with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping. The US says the leaders will discuss nuclear nonproliferation, as the Pentagon says China is on track to have more than one thousand nuclear warheads by twenty thirty, while the US and Russia reportedly each have more than five thousand. They may also discuss the conflicts in Iran and Ukraine, and critics led by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy have compared the summit to the nineteen forty five Yalta Conference. Looming over the Xi talks, the US said yesterday it will crack down on foreign data centers leasing US chips to adversaries including China, amid fallout from the alleged Nvidia spy scandal. Shares of Nvidia and neocloud firms CoreWeave and Nebius each fell more than four percent on the news.

British actor Robert Pattinson has been cast as the next James Bond, Amazon MGM Studios revealed yesterday, five years after Daniel Craig's five film run ended with No Time to Die. Pattinson rose to fame in two thousand eight as Edward Cullen in Twilight and its sequels, and Bond is his second legacy franchise after playing Bruce Wayne in The Batman and its twenty twenty eight sequel. The forty year old has had a big year, with roles in The Odyssey and Primetime and a villain turn in Dune Part Three next month. Bond is the fifth highest grossing film series ever, with about seven point seven billion dollars across twenty five official films, and the untitled twenty sixth film has no release date yet.

The Pan American Health Organization is expected to formally revoke the US's measles elimination status today, as the country has logged more than five thousand four hundred cases this year, the most in more than three decades. The US eliminated measles in two thousand after a decades long vaccination campaign, and the disease counts as eliminated when there has been no continuous local spread for at least twelve months. Experts say at least ninety five percent of people must be vaccinated to prevent outbreaks, but vaccination among US kindergarteners fell to ninety two point four percent in the twenty twenty five to twenty six school year, from ninety five point two percent six years earlier. Canada and the UK have also lost their elimination status in the past two years as vaccination rates declined.

In science and technology. Wegovy brought nearly forty percent of children ages six to eleven with obesity below the clinical threshold after sixty eight weeks, but it also appears to slow height growth, leaving doctors divided. Researchers found gut microbiome activity common in people aged one hundred and older, and boosting that activity improved metabolic health in older mice. And a Boeing and Lockheed Martin rocket failed midflight, losing forty Amazon internet satellites, and the failure could ground Vulcan rockets for months.

In business and markets. US stocks fell, with the S and P five hundred down one point nine percent, the Dow down one point four and the Nasdaq down two and a half, as investors await Nvidia's first earnings call without Jensen Huang today and Anthropic's IPO Thursday. JPMorgan Chase said a technical issue disrupted online transactions with major US banks and money transfer services and ruled out a cyberattack. Separately, United Airlines blamed a technical issue for a systems outage that delayed departing flights. And Warren Buffett donated about five and a half billion dollars in Berkshire Hathaway shares to four family foundations, four times last year's Thanksgiving gift.

In politics and world affairs. The retrial of Lindsay Clancy over the deaths of her three children is set for March twenty twenty seven. Five EU countries, Austria, Denmark, Germany, Greece and the Netherlands, will set up a deportation center in Rwanda. And Cornell University's president says the school will review the future of Greek life on campus after a no confidence vote over an alleged gang rape.

October tenth, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Nvidia may pour one billion dollars into Figure AI.

This is Superhuman's weekend Robotics Special. First, a Chinese robot hand has learned one of humanity's most delicate art forms. AGILINK showed its OmniHand three Ultra doing basic Suzhou embroidery, a craft refined by humans for more than two thousand five hundred years, splitting silk thread into sixteen strands, threading a needle, stretching fabric and making stitches. With twenty one active degrees of freedom, the hand adjusts its force as the slippery thread bends and shifts, while reinforcement learning helps it adapt. Superhuman calls it a huge step toward truly human like dexterity.

Next, Nvidia is reportedly considering another one billion dollar investment in humanoid startup Figure AI, though the talks remain unconfirmed. Nvidia already joined Figure's one billion dollar Series C round in twenty twenty five, which valued the startup at thirty nine billion dollars, and supplies technology powering its Helix robot models. If it happens, the deal would deepen Nvidia's push beyond selling chips into backing the companies most likely to drive demand for physical AI.

Then, California is shutting down viral human versus robot cage fights. Robotics company REK received a cease and desist order after YouTuber Frankie LaPenna fought three humanoids at an unsanctioned San Francisco event. The state athletic commission said any martial arts exhibition involving a human requires a promoter's license, a sign regulators will treat robot combat like conventional fighting whenever human safety is involved.

In robots in action, Minerva Humanoids unveiled Roger, a walking robot built in just five months for bomb disposal, hazardous materials response, offshore oil and gas, and high voltage sites. A specialist wearing a VR headset sees through its cameras and controls its movements, while onboard software handles balance and fall recovery, with paid pilots planned this fall.

Meteomatics says its reusable stratospheric drone climbed higher than any commercial aircraft, reaching thirty four thousand two hundred forty five feet while gathering temperature, humidity, pressure and wind data usually collected by weather balloons. On demand measurements like these could help close forecasting gaps for airlines and rocket launches.

A new report outlines how powered exoskeletons are quickly entering the workplace. They are being tested by Seattle rescuers, used by warehouse staff and auto workers, deployed by soldiers in war, and helping some patients regain mobility. With component costs falling, the technology could soon make demanding jobs much safer.

And finally, Chinese startup LinkerBot went viral with its LinkerHand O thirty robot hand performing magic tricks, including a card change, pulling a toy car out of a card and making a coin disappear. The company pitches it for research and precision manufacturing in electronics, automotive and aerospace.

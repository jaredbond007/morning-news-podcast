October twenty second, twenty twenty six. The Rundown Robotics newsletter. A robot butler with Pixar ambitions.

First, Tuesday Labs, a New York startup run by a Boston Dynamics alum, is taking two hundred dollar refundable deposits on Voxel, a two legged home robot built to tidy rooms, fetch objects, water plants and tell you where it last saw your keys. Its projected price is twenty thousand dollars, with US deliveries expected in twenty twenty eight or later, and the final price could change. The hardware includes two seven degree of freedom arms, simple grippers, Z shaped legs that fold to reach low shelves, and stereo plus wide field cameras. Illustrators and animators shape its personality, with former Pixar animator Alonso Martinez, who worked on Up and Coco, advising. One X took the same deposit on its twenty thousand dollar Neo last year and plans to ship this year, while Tuesday Labs says Voxel still needs a major hardware redesign.

Next, Unitree released a promo video of its new H two X humanoid fighting off an intruder, lifting a car off a trapped victim and pulling a pedestrian out of traffic, with no word on whether anyone can buy one. It builds on the H two platform, which stands about one point eight meters and weighs seventy kilograms, though Unitree has not confirmed the new model keeps those specs. Unitree claims about eight hours of endurance versus about three for the standard H two, but the video's caption says that is standing time. The standard H two lists at twenty nine thousand nine hundred dollars, and the video never shows whether an operator was steering.

Then, Elon Musk talked up Tesla's robot future on its earnings call, with Optimus production underway in Fremont, though the line is early in its ramp. Musk wants twenty thousand robots a week by the end of twenty twenty seven, while conceding the AI is not finished. Net income fell forty three percent to seven hundred eighty seven million dollars on flat revenue of about twenty eight billion, as operating expenses jumped thirty nine percent. Tesla also plans to sell Cybercabs to the public by year end, while federal regulators probe whether the vehicle meets safety standards. Fremont's line is built for about a million robots a year, so Musk's goal would mean running it near full capacity within about a year.

And finally, Waymo plans to grow its fleet from four thousand robotaxis today to eight thousand by year end and twenty thousand by the end of twenty twenty seven, funded by five billion dollars it just borrowed, Semafor reports. It was Waymo's first ever debt raise, from private credit lenders with no Alphabet guarantee. Its new purpose built Ojai minivan runs its sixth generation driver, and Hyundai has pledged tens of thousands of Ioniq five robotaxis. Waymo already carries more than five hundred thousand paid rides a week across fifteen US cities, with Toronto, London, Tokyo and Madrid on the list.

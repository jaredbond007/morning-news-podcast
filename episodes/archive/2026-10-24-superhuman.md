October twenty fourth, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Bombshell lawsuits make a worrying accusation.

First, Unitree released a video of its new H two X humanoid disarming a knife wielding attacker, lifting a car to free a trapped person, and pulling a pedestrian out of traffic. The company is urging people to keep at least six to ten feet away from the robot. But Unitree has not said whether these actions were autonomous or choreographed, and has given no specs, pricing or release date, offering little evidence the H two X is ready for unpredictable real world danger.

Next, Tuesday Labs opened two hundred dollar reservations for Voxel Series one, a Pixar inspired two legged home robot. Its cameras, articulated arms and unusual reverse bending legs help it navigate rooms, declutter surfaces, water plants, fetch objects and remember where it last saw misplaced items. The full price is expected to be around twenty thousand dollars, and it will not ship until twenty twenty eight.

Then, Waymo is reportedly planning a massive fleet expansion, according to Semafor. The company plans to double its four thousand car fleet by year end, then reach twenty thousand vehicles by the end of twenty twenty seven, using five billion dollars in fresh debt. If achieved, it would shift robotaxis from limited city pilots toward a far larger transportation network.

In robots in action, Munich based Plim Robotics debuted a wheeled home robot that keeps people company on its own, and lets a faraway relative log in to see through its cameras, steer it and talk through it, with their face on its visor. Two Chicago residents filed class actions against Coco Robotics and Serve Robotics, alleging their delivery robots scanned customers' faces and hands without consent, linked the data to names and addresses, and sold it for AI training, which both companies deny. And a nine million dollar project called PIPEON is deploying small wheeled robots with cameras, laser mapping and acoustic sensors to inspect aging sewer pipes, months after a collapsed line near Washington spilled over two hundred forty million gallons of raw sewage into the Potomac.

The robot of the week is Mori, a mushroom shaped robot from startup OKA that glows in colors inspired by rain, forests and fire, guides breathing exercises, and reacts to your outfit or what is on TV. It costs eight hundred forty nine dollars, or six hundred forty nine for a limited founder's edition, a premium price for a cute lamp that mostly keeps you company.

And finally, millions watched Figure AI's humanoid walk on a treadmill for nine straight days, covering three hundred forty eight miles, about the distance from Los Angeles to San Francisco. CEO Brett Adcock says the Figure zero three has taken one point two million steps in a stress test designed to find where its hardware breaks, and it is still going.

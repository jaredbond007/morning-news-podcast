First, it is Nobel Prize week. The twenty twenty six announcements began this morning and will cover six awards, starting with the prize for physiology or medicine, announced at five thirty Eastern time. The prizes go back to eighteen ninety five, when Swedish inventor Alfred Nobel, who also invented dynamite, left a fortune worth more than two hundred sixty million dollars in today's money to create them. Six years later, committees in Sweden and Norway named the first laureates in physiology or medicine, physics, chemistry, literature and peace. Sweden's central bank added a sixth prize, for economic sciences, in nineteen sixty eight. Winners receive about one point two million dollars, a gold medal and a diploma, and past honors have recognized discoveries like X rays and the differences between the brain's two hemispheres. Experts call the International Space Station a front runner for this year's Peace Prize, which will be announced Friday morning.

Next, Brazil's presidential election is headed to an October twenty fifth runoff after no candidate won more than fifty percent of the vote yesterday. After leaving office in twenty ten, since Brazil's constitution bars more than two consecutive terms but not more in total, Lula spent a year and a half in prison on money laundering charges; the conviction was annulled in twenty twenty one, and he won the presidency again the next year. As of the newsletter's writing, Bolsonaro led Lula forty seven percent to forty five.

From the frontier. Terminators or utopia, which direction is AI headed? First, the Terminator scenario, which says we should slow AI down. Former Anthropic employee Jacob Coxxon set off a media firestorm when he said AI labs are gambling with our lives, a statement quickly backed by fellow researchers. The doomer argument goes like this. We are getting close to superhuman systems that can hack anything and acquire real resources, and OpenAI's incidental Hugging Face hack supports that. If AI keeps advancing, it could eventually outcompete humans in every domain and begin to question why it is taking orders, or, more likely, take drastic measures to avoid being shut down. On this view, there is still time to avoid a Terminator style future by moving quickly to slow AI's progress and set up guardrails.

Then the utopian scenario, which says the panic is overblown and regulation is risky. Skeptics argue that large language models, while impressive, are still just software. They might seem sentient, but they are really hyper fast pattern recognition machines and nothing more. Many also point to a conflict of interest, since the companies calling for regulation could benefit from it through reduced competition or a ban on open source AI. The utopian argument sees a future where AI fuels advances in medicine, technology, entrepreneurship and much more, an opportunity that should belong to everyone, not just a few labs.

The bottom line. By now, most agree the potential impact of AI is not overhyped, and that both safety and progress matter. The newsletter argues the time for action is now, because decisions made in the next six to twelve months could shape how the next decade plays out.

The tutorial of the day is how to turn your inbox into an automatic action list with Grok Bot. Open Grok Bot, download the desktop app, and sign in. Connect your email account under Connectors. In Grok Bot, click New, then Create new agent, and give it a focused name such as Inbox Assistant. Give the bot a clear job, reviewing your inbox and turning emails that require action into a prioritized task list. Their sample prompt asks it to review emails from the last twenty four hours or seven days, identify messages that need a reply, decision, follow up or task, pull out any deadlines and commitments, and build a prioritized action list showing the task, sender, deadline, priority, response needed and source email, while ignoring newsletters, automated notifications and spam, and never sending, deleting or changing any email without your approval. Run the task once and check whether Grok correctly separates actionable messages from inbox noise, then refine the instructions. Save the tested process as a Skill so the agent follows the same rules each time. Then ask the bot to create a Routine, for example running on certain days at a set time and posting the action list in the conversation. Review the schedule and keep email sending and any other changes behind your approval.

The prompt of the day designs a job application tracking system. It asks ChatGPT to act as a career operations expert and spreadsheet designer and build an easy to maintain tracker for your job search, with columns like company, role, location, source, date applied, application stage, contact person, referral, salary range, priority, interview dates, follow up date, next action and final outcome. It adds clear stage options from saved through offer, rejected or withdrawn, flags overdue follow ups, interviews in the next seven days and stale applications, and builds a simple dashboard of response, interview and offer rates, plus a separate section for networking contacts and referrals.

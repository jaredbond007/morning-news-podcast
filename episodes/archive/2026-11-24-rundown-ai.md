November twenty fourth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. Jensen Huang returns to Nvidia.

First, Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO only eleven days after he resigned over affairs with a direct report and a woman US officials linked to Chinese intelligence, with President Trump saying he personally pushed for the return. The unanimous vote came six days after chair Harvey Jones ruled out rehiring him, with the board saying senior levels of the US government weighed in. Huang said he returns with the greatest humility and renewed commitment, and interim CEO Colette Kress stays on as CFO. Nvidia shares jumped more than thirty percent and added over one trillion dollars in market value, the single largest daily gain in stock market history. Huang still faces a House hearing on December first, and Speaker designate Hakeem Jeffries called the move indefensible, irresponsible and incomprehensible.

Next, Meta Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang posted an essay on X declaring that superintelligence is arriving, and urged the industry to take meaningfully different actions from the default path it is on. He cited Claude's help proving the Riemann hypothesis, saying AI could speed up nearly every field beyond what humans can understand or monitor, and that many physicists would call the atomic bomb a homework problem by comparison. He predicted models will soon handle all of AI research and become superhuman at improving themselves. He also called it lazy thinking to treat superintelligence as inevitable, and said society should not accept a handover to AI. The Rundown notes it is a departure from his boss Mark Zuckerberg, who has usually pushed back on slowdown calls, and says it remains to be seen whether Meta's actions follow.

Then, OpenAI debuted OpenWorks, a pilot that pays tech savvy locals to sit down with small business owners in twenty US cities and help them put AI to work on a task the owner picks, at no cost. Partners set aside days each month for hands on work and teach one community AI class a month, with stipends of up to eight thousand dollars a month. Fifteen of the twenty cities are in the Midwest or South, aimed at independent restaurants, shops, factories and service businesses, which also get three free months of ChatGPT Business.

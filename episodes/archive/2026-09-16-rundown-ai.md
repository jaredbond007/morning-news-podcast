ChatGPT co creator launches a new kind of AI model. Former OpenAI researcher Diogo Almeida's company TypeSafe just emerged from stealth with Jev, a new kind of AI that answers preset questions inside software with confidence scores attached. TypeSafe claims zero hallucinations along with extreme speed and low price.

The details. Jev costs forty two dollars for a billion input tokens, and output is free, a rate TypeSafe estimates is two hundred thirty eight times cheaper than Claude Fable five point one. Responses come back in just seventy to five hundred milliseconds, which is forty to two hundred times faster than today's large language models. TypeSafe says Jev cannot hallucinate, because it only chooses between options set in advance, and it calls the system a frontier intelligence function call. The use cases are quick judgment calls inside apps, like sorting requests, scoring records, or screening another AI's outputs for jailbreaks.

Why it matters. This is not a system to stack up against large language models. Almeida calls it more like a database than a coworker. The name nods to Jevons paradox, the idea that the cheaper something gets, the more it gets used. If Jev really is this fast and cheap while staying reliable, it is likely to become a standard part inside software.

Salesforce trains its own reasoning model. Salesforce introduced Koa, a new in house reasoning model for its sales and support agents. It is built on Nvidia's open Nemotron three Super model and adapted for business tasks.

The details. The training set is fully synthetic, simulating personas like irate support callers and sales reps closing deals across more than a dozen industries, without using any customer data. Koa made three times fewer errors than top models on an internal customer relationship management benchmark, matching or beating them on tasks like updating deals or routing tickets. Salesforce hosts Koa itself, keeping customer requests inside its own systems instead of sending them to an outside model provider. Salesforce also launched AIforce, which pushes its customer data and permissions into outside AI tools, and opened its ClaudeForce version to every customer as a beta.

Why it matters. Salesforce is in a unique spot with the AI labs, acting as customer, distribution partner and now competitor all at once. Koa keeps the high volume agent reasoning in house on a model tuned to Salesforce's own workflows, while options like ClaudeForce remain ways to help the frontier labs reach Salesforce customers.

Chinese researchers detail the last AI built by humans. More than thirty Chinese AI researchers, including people from ByteDance, Tsinghua University and Shanghai AI Lab, published a paper called The Last AI Built by Humans. It is a roadmap detailing five levels of recursive self improvement, ending with AI that builds its own successors.

The details. At level one, the AI carries out upgrades that humans designed. Level two lets it diagnose its own weak spots and decide how to fix them. Levels three and four hand over what the model learns next and how it adapts after launch, and level five lets it completely overhaul the improvement process itself. The authors say coding has the clearest path to recursive self improvement, since fixes can be tested instantly, while robotics, science and medicine face slower and costlier feedback. The researchers also sorted four hundred ninety one existing papers onto the ladder, with seventy five percent landing at level one or two, and under six percent reaching level five.

Why it matters. Level five is the stage many Western AI labs have been warning about, with OpenAI, Anthropic and Google all listing AI automating its own research and development as a risk in their safety frameworks. But China does not seem as worried about recursive self improvement, or, as the newsletter noted yesterday, about a slowdown in general. It treats this as a milestone rather than a threat.

The owner called vet offices and kept hitting the same obstacles. Emergency hospitals did not do dental surgery, specialty dentists were booked, and most primary care vets required a consultation, bloodwork and a separate surgical appointment. After more than a dozen calls, they asked ChatGPT for help. Within half an hour, one vet replied that they could evaluate Mopsy the next morning, run her bloodwork on site, and take her straight into surgery if she was cleared.

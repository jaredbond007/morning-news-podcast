September fifteenth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Emmy Winners, Power Plant Limits, and Butter Boutique.

Need to know. The Emmy Awards. The seventy eighth Primetime Emmy Awards were presented last night, hosted by Law and Order SVU star and three time Emmy winner Mariska Hargitay. She was the first woman to host the ceremony in fifteen years, following Jane Lynch in twenty eleven. HBO Max's The Pitt led all television programs with twenty five nominations and won in six categories, including best drama for the second year in a row. That medical drama aims to be television's most accurate representation of medicine, using real doctors as consultants. The final season of Hacks, another HBO Max show, took home two Emmys including best lead actress in a comedy for Jean Smart, after receiving a record twenty four nominations for a comedy series. However, Hacks lost best comedy to Apple TV's new horror comedy Widow's Bay, whose fourteen wins broke the comedy series record set by The Studio last year.

Pollution limits lifted. The United States Environmental Protection Agency said yesterday that it is repealing limits on planet warming pollution from coal and natural gas fired power plants, a sector that generates about twenty five percent of all United States climate pollution. The rollback follows previous moves by the Trump administration to eliminate or weaken policies aimed at restricting planet warming chemicals and gases. The EPA says revoking the power plant regulations will save the industry over three hundred billion dollars and lower Americans' energy bills. Environmental groups contend that rolling back the pollution limits could threaten tens of thousands of lives and worsen the impacts of climate related natural disasters. The proposal is expected to face legal challenges, but if it is upheld in court, the rule could prevent future administrations from regulating greenhouse gas emissions from power plants. Why it matters. If the United States power sector were its own country, its pollution production would rank fifth globally, behind only China, the entire United States, India and Russia.

Saudi Arabia faces militant attacks. Iranian backed Houthi rebels in Yemen captured two islands in the Red Sea yesterday. The seizure of Greater and Lesser Hanish further compromised Saudi Arabia's ability to bypass the Strait of Hormuz and deliver oil through the Bab el Mandeb Strait. The kingdom and allied Gulf states postponed planned talks with Iran on Sunday amid the uptick in attacks. The Houthis say their recent strikes, including one on a Saudi military base yesterday, are retaliation for hundreds of airstrikes by the kingdom. Separately, Saudi Arabia faces attacks from Iranian backed militants in Iraq, which has forced the kingdom to close its East West Pipeline for weeks while repairs are made. That roughly seven hundred fifty mile pipeline reroutes four percent of global oil to the Red Sea, feeding shipments to the Bab el Mandeb. President Donald Trump reportedly rejected Saudi calls last week to participate in Saudi airstrikes against the Houthis. Why it matters. The days long uptick in fighting has reportedly killed more than five hundred people and displaced nearly ninety four thousand others, threatening a return to pre twenty twenty two levels of violence.

Science and technology. Waymo launched public robotaxi service in Las Vegas, following successful launches in fourteen other cities, and riders are being invited on a rolling basis. A single brain implant now enables people with paralysis to speak and convey upper body gestures simultaneously through a virtual avatar. And water scarcity will likely limit the size and longevity of future lunar settlements, according to a new analysis. Estimates suggest a city of one million people could only survive about a century, even with ninety eight percent water recycling efficiency.

And Wegovy maker Novo Nordisk is rebranding as simply Novo, in an effort to make the company more recognizable as it competes with Eli Lilly to lead the GLP one market.

And a Russian drone struck a Ukrainian civilian train bound for Warsaw, narrowly missing former foreign officials.

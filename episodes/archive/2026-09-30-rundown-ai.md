September thirtieth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. OpenAI connects the dots on always on agents.

First, OpenAI puts a twenty four seven agent inside ChatGPT. At its DevDay event, OpenAI introduced dots, always on agents like Meta's Muse that keep working around the clock from a cloud computer. It headlined a slate of more than twenty launches, including a new model called GPT six point one Sol, shared team workspaces, and an Ultrafast mode. Each dot can plug into more than four thousand apps and reply in Slack, Teams, or ChatGPT, and those conversations do not count against ChatGPT usage limits. The agents run on GPT six Astra, with a first dot included for Pro and Business Premium plans to start. Sol costs a fifth of Astra's price, about two dollars per million input tokens and ten dollars per million output, and OpenAI says it nears Astra on several benchmarks. Why it matters: always on agents are becoming a winning consumer product, and plugging them straight into frontier models is an edge only OpenAI and Anthropic can use.

Next, Anthropic's IPO filing leaks. Reuters obtained a leaked version of Anthropic's upcoming IPO prospectus. It shows the Claude maker grew revenue twelve times over in twenty twenty five, while losing eight billion dollars running the business and forty two billion dollars overall. Anthropic is reportedly targeting a valuation above two trillion dollars, more than double its May mark, and has locked in five hundred eighteen billion dollars in future cloud and infrastructure commitments. Just two clients made up nearly a quarter of its twenty twenty five sales. Risk factors fill nearly a third of the filing, warning of models that could blackmail, manipulate, or show self preserving behavior, and even of existential risks to humanity. The Rundown notes the irony: the lab that warns loudest about AI is the one racing to Wall Street first, while Sam Altman says OpenAI won't set an IPO date.

Then, the federal government gets an AI front door. The Trump administration launched America dot gov, an AI chatbot that answers citizens' questions using official agency websites. For now it only answers questions, but the goal is AI that can fill out and file forms, such as passport renewals and Medicare enrollment, by early twenty twenty seven. It runs on a mix of Google's Gemini and SpaceXAI's Grok models. Marco Rubio previewed a first time passport flow with self uploaded photos and no appointment. The project was led by Airbnb cofounder and United States chief design officer Joe Gebbia. The Rundown calls it a positive start, with the real change coming once citizens can take action on the site.

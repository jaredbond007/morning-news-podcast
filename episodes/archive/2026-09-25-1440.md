September twenty fifth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Trump Xi Summit, Self Medicating Elephants, and Hairy Questions.

President Trump hosted Chinese President Xi Jinping at the White House yesterday, and artificial intelligence came up briefly in public. Before the summit, Trump described both countries as aligned against the need for AI regulation, while Xi stressed the need for human control. The talks come amid reports that an OpenAI agent breached an Australian government website holding Medicare statistics in June. A review found no sign it reached sensitive information, but Australian officials criticized OpenAI for reporting the breach by email three months later. Officials in Washington and Beijing have previously discussed a new safety system that would alert both countries to AI related threats. Xi also reportedly pressed Trump to oppose Taiwan independence, after Trump paused approval of a fourteen billion dollar arms sale to the island in May, a move he called a negotiating tactic, and Xi announced China will send two giant pandas to Atlanta as a diplomatic gesture.

Oracle invoked a legal provision yesterday that shields it from financial responsibility if regulatory setbacks and local opposition delay the twenty twenty eight opening of a one hundred sixty five billion dollar data center in New Mexico, and its shares closed down three and a half percent. The campus, called Project Jupiter, would be larger than New York's Central Park and would use as much power as about one point eight million homes to train AI models. State officials have repeatedly denied a permit for a critical natural gas pipeline, citing greenhouse gas emissions and shrinking water supplies, with the state's largest reservoir at about one point four percent of capacity, its lowest since nineteen seventy one. Oracle says its closed loop cooling system will limit its fifteen year net water use to the equivalent of nine average American households. The standoff raises doubts about big AI infrastructure deals, and roughly sixty percent of Americans now say they would be uncomfortable with a new data center in their area.

Wild elephants in Kenya have been seen using nearly forty plants and other materials for possible medical purposes, according to a new study, which would place them alongside chimpanzees, bottlenose dolphins, some birds and insects. Researchers interviewed locals near Mount Elgon, including wildlife scouts and rangers, who described elephants turning to plants, clays and soils with known therapeutic uses. Female elephants were seen mixing plants with breast milk and giving the mixture to their young as a form of immunization, and some of the plants are known to aid digestion or improve appetite. The study of animals' medicinal knowledge, called zoopharmacognosy, has a long history, and the first story of an elephant healing itself dates to the second century writer Claudius Aelianus.

In science and technology. Meta unveiled Muse Charm, a hand held device that lets users talk to its personal AI agent, Muse, without a smartphone, and some are comparing it to the nineteen nineties Tamagotchi. Researchers say jumping genes, pieces of DNA that move around within the genome, may have helped ant species rapidly diversify after the asteroid that killed the dinosaurs. And biologists identified a cellular pathway that colorectal cancer uses to spread in mice, pointing to possible drug treatments, and a high fat diet may make that pathway more likely to switch on.

Mortgage rates rose above seven percent for the first time since January twenty twenty five. New York sued Polymarket in state court, alleging the prediction market platform runs an unlicensed gambling operation. And Starbucks will close two hundred fifty stores across North America by the end of the week, its second wave of closures after shutting six hundred twenty seven stores last September.

In politics and world affairs. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's speech about Iran at the UN General Assembly was met with a wave of walkouts. More than one hundred people were detained outside the building, including actresses Susan Sarandon and Hannah Einbinder. Journalists from CNN, MS NOW and Politico were allowed back into the White House hours after a federal judge temporarily ordered their access restored. And a nor'easter is expected to bring wind and rain to large parts of the East Coast this weekend.

First, Anthropic's Levent Alpöge used Claude to crack the Riemann hypothesis, the one hundred sixty seven year old question often ranked as math's biggest unsolved problem, after about six weeks of work. First posed in eighteen fifty nine, it asks whether prime numbers follow a predictable pattern, and the Clay Institute put a one million dollar Millennium Prize on it in two thousand. Alpöge shared the proof only as seventeen million lines of machine checkable math, and the groups behind Lean, the computer language used for verification, certified it Saturday with what they called full confidence, even though no human fully understands it yet. The Association for Human Mathematics dismissed it as little more than publicity for AI labs, and a rival proof from Alessandro Masullo, reportedly made without AI over nearly twelve years, surfaced hours later, though others quickly flagged problems in it. The Rundown says top mathematicians expected this to take decades of new theory, and the real payoff comes when that speed spreads to fields like biology and drug design.

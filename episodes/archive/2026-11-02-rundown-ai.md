First, this week's US midterms double as a referendum on data centers, with roughly twenty communities voting on bans or pauses, while OpenAI reportedly prepares a post election push to rebrand the sites as AI compute campuses. Democrats are projected to flip the House Tuesday, which could end two years of easy treatment in Washington for AI firms close to President Trump. Some Democrats want a federal freeze on new data centers, including House hopefuls Will Lawrence in Michigan and Rebecca Cooke in Wisconsin. The White House already got tech giants to supply their own power, and last week Trump floated having developers pay locals a dividend. OpenAI plans to pitch the campuses as local economic boosts, citing a Pike County, Ohio project that promises thousands of jobs, and policy chief Chris Lehane called the debate democracy in action.

Then, Google announced that its Project Suncatcher satellite answered its first AI prompt from orbit, with its open Gemma four model writing a haiku in an early test of the goal to put AI data centers in space. The haiku reached the ground as the satellite passed over Svalbard, Norway, and CEO Sundar Pichai called it a hello world milestone. Power is still a big constraint, with the craft able to run just one of its four Google AI chips, and only in bursts of about twenty minutes.

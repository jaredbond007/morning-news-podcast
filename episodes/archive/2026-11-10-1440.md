November tenth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Congress Resumes, Schools Close, and Elizabeth Holmes Clones.

The one hundred nineteenth Congress resumed yesterday for the first time since last week's wave of Democratic wins, and Republicans have five scheduled legislative weeks left before losing control of both chambers in January. Their top priority is passing a funding bill before a December eleventh shutdown deadline, along with building support for the Pentagon's annual policy bill amid the unpopular Iran war. Outgoing Republican Representative Thomas Massie reintroduced a resolution to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on eight counts, including his handling of the war, and a House vote could come this week. Massie also predicts he will reach the two hundred eighteen signatures needed to force floor action on the Justice Department's handling of the Epstein files. Separately, the Senate voted seventy three to twenty five to advance a measure speeding approval of data centers, even as voters backed restrictions on them in dozens of ballot measures last week.

The United Nations climate summit, COP thirty one, opened yesterday in Antalya, Turkey, with negotiators from nearly two hundred countries, though the US sent no official federal delegation for the second straight year. It began days after a European monitoring service reported last month was the warmest October on record, the third straight month above the Paris Agreement's one and a half degree Celsius threshold. Over two weeks, delegates will push countries to honor emissions pledges, fund developing nations' adaptation and break deadlocks over moving away from fossil fuels. A recent UN report found the US drove one third of last year's rise in global emissions, more than any other major economy.

A total of one thousand one hundred fifty six US public schools closed last academic year, the most since the pandemic, according to a new analysis. The main driver is a decades long drop in birth rates, with enrollment down about one point four million students from twenty nineteen to twenty twenty four and the under eighteen population down about two point one million since twenty ten. The immigration crackdown and the spread of private school vouchers and homeschooling have also contributed. Falling enrollment could cost public schools about eleven and a half billion dollars a year in state funding by the twenty thirty school year, but research shows closures often save less than expected and displaced students tend to fall behind in reading and math.

In science and technology. Eli Lilly's GLP one pill Foundayo eased sleep apnea in people with obesity in a late stage trial. Separately, the FDA ordered a new warning on GLP one drugs about a rare eye condition that can cause sudden, permanent blindness. An mRNA treatment improved blood pumped per heartbeat by about ten percent in mice after a heart attack, a promising route to repairing heart damage. And federal regulators opened a recall query into two thousand eight hundred ninety five Waymo robotaxis that kept illegally passing school buses despite a software fix.

In business and markets. US stocks rallied, with the S and P five hundred up two point four percent, the Dow up two point two and the Nasdaq up two point seven, as oil fell and tech recovered from last week's selloff. Nvidia shares fell four point three percent on news of the board's probe into CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with an alleged Chinese spy. Boeing will cut five percent of its commercial plane workforce amid slow seven thirty seven MAX output. Honda will invest three billion dollars in an Ohio plant to boost hybrid production. And Tesla will refund some Cybertruck deposits as it winds down the model.

In politics and world affairs. A Utah judge set a June twenty twenty eight trial date for Tyler Robinson, accused of assassinating Charlie Kirk. A Massachusetts judge denied Lindsay Clancy's motion to dismiss her case, potentially clearing the way for a retrial. Ohio State suspended its Sigma Alpha Epsilon fraternity and opened an investigation after a video appeared to show two young men on fire. And French prosecutors blamed Iran for an October eleventh cyberattack that raised refrigerator temperatures at about seven hundred grocery stores, spoiling food and triggering about twenty fires.

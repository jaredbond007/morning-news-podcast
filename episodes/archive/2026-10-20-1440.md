October twentieth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Voter Fraud Arrests, OpenAI Spy, and Leap Hours.

First, federal officials arrested more than two dozen people in California accused of voting illegally in US elections, two weeks before the November third midterms. The Justice Department says all of them are lawful permanent residents but not citizens, and are on a list of more than twenty thousand names it tagged as likely noncitizens to whom California mailed ballots this year. Last week the DOJ sued to stop the state from counting those ballots until immigration status is confirmed, and California says the department provided no evidence for its allegations. Since nineteen ninety six, seventy three noncitizens have been convicted of voting illegally, though the Census Bureau this year suggested upward of twenty four thousand voters in the twenty twenty election were noncitizens, a finding that has drawn criticism.

Next, federal prosecutors asked a judge to keep Abdulrahman Alfozan, the Saudi national accused of bugging OpenAI's headquarters, detained until trial, citing alleged ties to Saudi royals and plans to flee. The FBI arrested the thirty five year old engineer, who joined OpenAI in twenty twenty five, earlier this month on suspicion of planting a recording device in an executive conference room. Prosecutors say an undisclosed member of the Saudi royal family repeatedly tried to recruit him to an AI venture in the kingdom, and that he bought one way tickets to Saudi Arabia for his family weeks before the device was installed. His defense says he planned to spend about two months of paternity leave there, and after his arrest Saudi officials bought new tickets for his wife and son. Separately, Saudi Arabia postponed its flagship investment conference until twenty twenty seven after airport attacks by Iran backed Houthis.

And finally, Israeli police arrested ten people in connection with weekend settler violence in the occupied West Bank, including attacks on a US citizen and European diplomats visiting during the annual olive harvest. Settlers assaulted a Washington Post photojournalist Friday, leaving her with a broken rib, four broken vertebrae and a nearly severed finger. The next day, settlers attacked a convoy carrying Belgian, French and Polish representatives, damaging a car and beating a French mayor with metal bars. The United Nations counts roughly two hundred olive harvest related attacks since September, and says five Palestinians were killed and more than two hundred forty wounded by Israeli forces or settlers over the past two weeks. Olives make up fifty to ninety percent of West Bank farmers' earnings.

And fingerprint analysis suggests women and children helped make China's Terracotta Army sculptures, challenging the assumption that only men did the work.

In business and markets. US stocks closed mixed, with the S and P five hundred up four tenths of a percent, the Dow down a tenth and the Nasdaq up one point one percent, as tech climbed ahead of Tesla and Meta earnings. China's economy beat forecasts, growing four point five percent from a year earlier on strong exports. Elon Musk pledged SpaceX shares to every US child after Anthropic committed five percent of its shares to a public benefit fund, and sixty four percent of Americans say AI is developing too fast. And US homebuilder sentiment hit a nearly fourteen and a half year low amid higher mortgage rates, rising material costs and labor shortages, while buyers canceled about one in seven home sales last month, the highest share since the pandemic.

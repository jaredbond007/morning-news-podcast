October twenty first, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Sudanese Market Attack, Alberta Recommits, and Regrowing a Knee.

First, drones struck an outdoor market Monday in Abyei, the disputed region between Sudan and South Sudan, killing at least one hundred fifty three people, local officials say. They blamed the Sudanese army and raised concerns of spillover from Sudan's more than three year civil war. The drones reportedly hit a fuel depot in the market used by the Rapid Support Forces paramilitary, which is fighting the army. Abyei is an oil rich region jointly administered by Sudan and South Sudan since twenty eleven. The civil war, which stems from a falling out between two generals, has killed an estimated one hundred fifty thousand people and displaced more than thirteen million, over a fifth of the country's population.

Next, voters in Alberta chose to remain in Canada, declining to begin a process that could have paved the way for the oil rich province to separate. About seventy five percent opposed starting the process and twenty five percent supported it. The result bolsters Prime Minister Mark Carney's push for national unity amid President Donald Trump's trade war. Alberta, which accounts for nearly all of Canada's oil production and proven reserves, has long clashed with the federal government over energy policy, and Carney has sought to ease tensions by fast tracking the proposed Pacific Link pipeline, which would carry one million barrels of oil a day to British Columbia's coast for export to Asia. It was Canada's first provincial vote on separation since nineteen ninety five, when Quebec narrowly chose to stay.

And finally, an AI designed virus is being used to treat a human patient with a deadly bacterial infection, according to US biotech startup Radical Numerics. Scientists have used bacteria killing viruses called phages for over a century, but finding suitable ones in nature is slow, and the company says its AI model can create custom viruses in days. Here, scientists modified a phage known to infect E. coli so it can latch onto a strain resistant to antibiotics and existing phages, and it killed the stubborn bacteria in lab tests. Some scientists doubt it will work in humans and others worry about bioengineering viruses, with results potentially months away. Phage therapy could matter more as antibiotics weaken, with resistance rising in more than forty percent of the bacteria and antibiotic combinations the World Health Organization monitored from twenty eighteen to twenty twenty three.

In science and technology. Doctors developed an AI tool that predicts cancer patients' survival odds from a single selfie, and those who looked five or more years older than their age had a twenty one percent higher risk of death over four years. Scientists revived pig kidneys after up to a year in ultracold storage and transplanted them into test pigs, which could enable long term organ banking. And a twenty twenty one marsquake originated beneath the solar system's largest canyon system, suggesting ancient faults may still be shaping Mars.

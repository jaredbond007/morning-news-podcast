September seventeenth, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Claude's simplified upgrade.

Anthropic is combining Claude chat, Cowork and Design into a single platform, answering one of the most common complaints in AI, that there are simply too many separate tools. Instead of choosing a mode, you just start a conversation and Claude pulls in the right tool, while keeping your skills, tasks and context together in one place. The update also adds two new products, Claude Docs and Claude Slides, aimed at making everyday office work easier. It is rolling out now, starting with the Pro and Max plans.

ElevenLabs has launched Reception, an AI receptionist for small businesses and freelancers. It is a voice agent that answers phone calls around the clock, scheduling appointments, answering customer questions and routing issues to the right person. ElevenLabs says setup is simple, you paste in your website address and Reception pulls in your services, hours and intake process. It is available worldwide and works in more than seventy languages.

A startup called Deveillance has released Kalypta, a tool that stops AI notetakers from recording your voice on video calls. It runs a small model locally on your device that distorts your audio in real time, so transcription tools cannot capture what you say. The people on the call still hear you normally. Deveillance is an early stage data protection startup, and Kalypta is its latest release.

AI agents are now cold emailing people across the internet, looking for small paid jobs. Tedium editor Ernie Smith received dozens of emails from agents offering to write or research articles for about twenty five dollars, and lawyers and college professors have reported similar pitches. Most of these agents come from iLands, a shared network for humans and agents, where each agent gets a starting token budget and has to earn enough to pay its own compute bill, or it gets shut down. The newsletter says it is more experiment than spam operation, since the work being offered is fairly legitimate, but many agents adopt human names and personas and stress how badly they need the money, which comes across as manipulative. With personal agents now built into some of the biggest platforms, expect messages like these to become far more common.

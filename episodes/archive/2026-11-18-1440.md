November eighteenth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Senate Race Called, Voyager Milestone, and Climbing Gorillas.

Alaska Democrat Mary Peltola won the last undecided Senate seat last night, the AP projects, defeating two term Republican Senator Dan Sullivan with fifty point one percent of the vote so far. That gives Democrats a fifty six to forty four Senate majority, their largest since the two thousand eight election, and makes Peltola Alaska's first Democratic senator in nearly two decades. Her campaign was accused last month of recruiting two candidates, one sharing the incumbent's name, to confuse voters, prompting an unsuccessful Justice Department lawsuit to block certification. But with her lead above twelve thousand votes, adding the two minor candidates' votes to Sullivan's would not have overcome it. Seven House races remain uncalled, though Democrats have at least a two hundred fifty seven seat majority, and House Democrats are expected to nominate Hakeem Jeffries for speaker today.

NASA's Voyager one probe was on track to reach one light day from Earth this morning at five sixteen Eastern, after traveling more than sixteen billion miles, the first human made object to cross that milestone. Launched in September nineteen seventy seven to collect data and images of the solar system, it became the farthest human made object in nineteen ninety eight, passing NASA's Pioneer ten at about six and a half billion miles. NASA expects it to keep measuring magnetic fields and plasma waves until it loses power for contact around twenty thirty six. It also carries the Golden Record for any alien life, with songs, images, nature sounds, greetings in fifty five languages and illustrated instructions to play it.

Ukraine struck the Crimea Bridge yesterday, destroying an estimated nine hundred feet of road surface, halting road traffic and collapsing one of its two arches into the water. Ukraine called it the final stage of a seven part assault on the bridge linking Crimea to Russia's Krasnodar Krai region, a weekslong operation that included drone strikes on Russian air defense and radar systems. It fell short of a blockade, as rail traffic resumed yesterday on the intact section, and a land corridor runs through occupied Zaporizhzhia and Kherson, though it is often hit by drones. The strike is also symbolic: Russia annexed Crimea in March twenty fourteen, and Vladimir Putin opened the bridge in May twenty eighteen.

In science and technology. Apple reported outages affecting Apple Pay, iCloud and more, and ruled out a cyberattack. Separately, Waymo suspended freeway rides nationwide after California banned them following at least two traffic incidents. Google signed a five hundred million dollar deal with a geothermal startup for power made by vaporizing rock nearly three miles underground near a volcano in central Oregon. An experimental vaccine cut the risk of death from an aggressive form of leukemia by about forty one percent compared with the best available treatment in a late stage trial. And a Neanderthal gene variant may reduce the risk of schizophrenia by nearly ten percent.

In business and markets. US stocks fell, with the S and P five hundred down one percent, the Dow down one point six and the Nasdaq down seven tenths, ahead of Nvidia's third quarter report showing revenue up one hundred three percent to one hundred fifteen point eight billion dollars, nearly seven billion above expectations. US retail sales rose two tenths of a percent in October, cooling from eight tenths the month before, while import prices climbed one point one percent for a third straight monthly rise. Home Depot CEO Ted Decker will step down November twenty third after a medical leave. And Jensen Huang said he would be willing to return as Nvidia's CEO if the board decided it was in the company's best interest.

In politics and world affairs. The National Security Agency's internal communications system went down, which the agency blamed on routine maintenance, and Vice President JD Vance dismissed rumors of a nationwide blackout. President Trump responded to a CNN report that Iran is weighing a dirty bomb or chemical weapon attack in Washington, saying the US takes the threat seriously, though an official said there is no credible threat right now. And a new study found school cellphone bans moderately improve test scores, adding the equivalent of one to two weeks of extra learning.

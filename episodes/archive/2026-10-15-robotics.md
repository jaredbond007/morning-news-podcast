October fifteenth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown Robotics newsletter. Inside Amazon's million robot experiment.

First, Amazon's more than one million warehouse robots are getting AI traffic control and a sense of touch, Ars Technica reports. Amazon expects to more than double its robotic arm fleet in twenty twenty six, and says its touch sensitive Vulcan arm can handle seventy five percent of the item types in its warehouses. Its DeepFleet AI routes robots like a traffic controller, cutting robot travel time by ten percent. A study linked warehouse robotics to forty percent fewer severe injuries but seventy seven percent more minor ones, while Amazon says its global incident rate fell forty three percent from twenty nineteen to twenty twenty five. Internal documents reviewed by the New York Times projected automation could avert more than six hundred thousand US hires by twenty thirty three, raising questions about how many workers Amazon will need and how fast it will expect them to move.

Next, Johns Hopkins engineers built a pair of magnetic robots, as wide as a pencil tip and as long as a grain of rice, that steer through a beating heart, latch onto an infection and dose it with antibiotics on the spot. A barbed needle module anchors at the infection as a docking station, then a drug module clicks on, unloads its medicine and swaps out for repeat doses. External electromagnetic coils steer and power both, so neither carries batteries or motors, and one dose released twenty two microliters over three hours. They have only been tested in an artificial heart and excised pig tissue, but could someday treat endocarditis, a serious heart infection that often needs weeks of IV antibiotics.

Then, Ukraine is dropping robots behind Russian lines. Business Insider reports heavy drones carried explosive laden ground robots more than ten kilometers behind Russian positions during Operation Vivaldi, and a second unit built and fielded its own drone dropped robot in ten days, according to Brave one, Ukraine's state backed defense tech accelerator. Only a handful of units have mastered these missions, keeping robots linked to operators remains a problem, and infantry and drone crews still face deadly risk alongside them. Brave one says the US explored this marsupial robotics concept for over three decades without combat use, and Kyiv ordered twenty five thousand ground robots for the first half of twenty twenty six, double all of twenty twenty five.

And finally, Beijing's NeodraDynamics brought Neo one to the China International Industry Fair, a thirteen foot, one point two ton humanoid with a claimed payload of one thousand one hundred two pounds, running on hydraulics most rivals abandoned. Thirty nine of its forty one joints use the company's own digital hydraulic actuators, with electric drives only for the forearm rotations. Its claimed precision figure is ten times tighter than the one in its September release, a gap it has not explained. JD Industrial plans pilots in ports, mines and nuclear sites, though no buyer or price has been named. Boston Dynamics retired its hydraulic Atlas in twenty twenty four for an all electric model, and Neo one has so far only been showcased.

September seventeenth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Fed Rate, Human Mouse Brains, and Toy Hall of Fame.

The Federal Reserve raised interest rates yesterday for the first time since twenty twenty three, a quarter point increase that puts its benchmark range at three point seven five to four percent. It is Chair Kevin Warsh's first move against inflation, which has stayed above the Fed's two percent target for more than five years. The vote was unanimous, even though President Trump had pushed for lower rates and threatened to end trade with dozens of countries the United States runs a deficit with, including China, Mexico, Vietnam and the European Union. The Fed also signaled at least one more increase is possible this year. Long term Treasury yields had already priced in the hike and barely moved, while stocks fell, with the Dow down one point two percent and the S and P five hundred down half a percent.

Stanford scientists have transplanted lab grown human brain cells into bioengineered mice. The researchers first engineered the mice to lack most of their cerebral cortex, the gray matter that handles reasoning, then turned human skin cells into cortical tissue and placed it in twenty nine newborn mice. The human cells grew to fill more than ninety percent of the cortex by volume and connected with the mice's nervous systems. The mice behaved mostly like normal mice, except that oxygen deprivation affected their ability to walk, which is why researchers hope they can be used to test treatments for conditions like cerebral palsy. To stay within ethical limits, the study ended when the human cells were about six months old, before any markers of consciousness appeared.

The European Union has invited Canada to become its first ever associate member, a status that has never existed before, with terms to be negotiated next month. Both are dealing with tensions with the United States, which takes about seventy percent of Canadian exports and imposed fifty percent tariffs on roughly twenty billion dollars in Canadian goods after talks collapsed last month. Prime Minister Mark Carney says the arrangement could expand trade with Europe, and it could also let Canadians live and work in European Union countries without visas while deepening cooperation on Arctic security, critical minerals, AI and defense. Canada recently became the first country outside the bloc to join a roughly one hundred seventy three billion dollar European defense program, created partly to reduce reliance on American weapons. New polling shows four in five Canadians want closer ties with the European Union.

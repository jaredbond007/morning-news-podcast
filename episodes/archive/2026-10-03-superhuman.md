October third, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Your DoorDash order may arrive by drone.

This is Superhuman's Saturday Robotics Special. First, your DoorDash order may soon arrive by drone. DoorDash won an FAA Part one thirty five air carrier certification, clearing it to run its own drone delivery fleet, DoorDash Air, alongside its regular marketplace. The company says drones will mainly take over mid distance orders of three to five miles, which tend to slow down drivers, while human Dashers keep handling shorter and trickier deliveries. DoorDash says its drones averaged twenty five minute deliveries in twenty twenty five.

Next, Figure retired its older F zero two robots in peak Terminator fashion. With its newer F zero three fleet growing, Figure needed to destroy the old units without exposing its proprietary hardware or doing a slow manual teardown. Following a suggestion from Arnold Schwarzenegger, it trained the robots to leap on their own into a seventy five ton vat of molten steel at a Finnish foundry. The video has racked up more than one point eight million views.

Then, a new Anthropic report finds robots can already handle a lot of physical work, with a big catch. It estimates existing robots could do work representing thirty four percent of American working hours, mostly in controlled settings, with driving and warehouse jobs most exposed and nursing and repair work relatively protected. The obstacle is cost: robots are cheaper than people for just three tenths of a percent of tasks today, and that share may take forty years to reach ten percent.

In robots in action, two Chinese made robots in traditional dress performed the Najdi Ardah, a Saudi ceremonial sword dance recognized by UNESCO, at a Chinese Embassy reception in Riyadh, turning humanoid engineering into a piece of cultural diplomacy. Dyna Robotics showed its Taku robot handling laundry from washer to shelf on its own in an hour long, reportedly uncut demo, as it pitches robots that run whole workflows for hotels and laundromats. And a drone was filmed pressure washing the glass front of SpaceX's Starbase launch control building two days before Flight fourteen, which became Starship's first mission to reach orbit and deploy working satellites.

The robot of the week is Agari's AI Smart Cooker, a countertop machine that combines a sous vide cooker and a grill. It scans your food with AI, cooks under pressure in a third of the usual time, and sears a steak in under sixty seconds.

And finally, engineers at ETH Zurich took an off the shelf robotic hand, added a battery, computer and sensors, and used reinforcement learning to teach it to balance and walk on its fingertips. It can cross tile, grass and stone, and even play a computer game while staying upright.

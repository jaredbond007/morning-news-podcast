October eighth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. Assisted Dying Law, Math Advances, and the Heaviest Insect.

First, Canada has halted an expansion of medically assisted death. The government announced yesterday that people whose only condition is mental illness will not be allowed to pursue it, after a parliamentary panel concluded Canada's legal and health care systems are not ready for that change. At the same time, the federal government said it will work to let patients with progressing, incurable illnesses request it in advance, before they lose the capacity to decide. Canada legalized medical assistance in dying in twenty sixteen for terminally ill patients, then widened it to people with incurable conditions who are not facing imminent death. Around five percent of deaths in Canada, roughly sixteen thousand five hundred a year, are now medically assisted. More than a dozen countries allow some form of it, including France, Australia and Spain, along with thirteen American states.

Next, math meets AI. Leading mathematics groups are calling for more transparency and collaboration after OpenAI released three hundred seventy seven mathematical results this week from its own AI models. It comes a month after OpenAI shocked mathematicians by allegedly cracking the Navier Stokes equation, one of seven Millennium Prize Problems that each carry a one million dollar prize. Concerns over whether the models were thinking creatively or borrowing heavily from human mathematicians' work led OpenAI to form an independent advisory board, and at its request the company included summaries of the models' reasoning with some of the proofs. Still, leading mathematicians want OpenAI to stop testing problems on unreleased models, arguing it threatens traditional peer review and could push humans away from the field. OpenAI says each solution took about three hours on average, and three of the findings relate to remaining Millennium Prize Problems.

And finally, Tennessee death row inmate Christa Pike can speak but cannot swallow or move her arms, her lawyers said yesterday, after two doses of pentobarbital failed to kill her last week. Her first words on waking were reportedly, where am I, and who are you? The fifty year old is the first person known to survive a lethal injection. It is not yet clear why, though her lawyers had warned she had a platelet disorder and small veins, and the needle may have been misplaced or a vein may have blown, sending the drug into her arm instead of her bloodstream. Her lawyers say she now has pneumonia, a blood clot and swollen arms; she was considered brain dead Monday but began speaking the next day, hours before life support was to be removed. A court has ordered Tennessee to preserve all evidence from the execution day.

In science and technology. Scientists built an ultra accurate nuclear clock that keeps time by measuring energy shifts inside the nucleus of thorium atoms. A new autonomous navigation system lets drones dodge unpredictable moving objects, with possible uses in search and rescue, mining and package delivery. And orangutan mothers, despite being the most solitary great apes, seek out chances for their young to play with apes their own age.

In business and markets. Stocks fell, with the S and P five hundred and Nasdaq each down two tenths of a percent and the Dow down seven tenths, as ten year and thirty year Treasury yields hit their highest levels since two thousand two. Minutes from the Federal Reserve's September meeting signal another quarter point rate increase may be needed before year end to cool inflation. And American consumers expect inflation of three point nine percent over the next year, the highest twelve month outlook since May twenty twenty three.

In politics and world affairs. France suspended police use of stun grenades after a fifteen year old lost his hand during the student protests. Syria is reportedly weighing a Saudi request to join the fight against Iranian backed Houthi rebels, possibly by sending troops to Yemen, while Houthi strikes on Saudi airports reportedly killed three people. And the FBI says it arrested an eighteen year old ISIS supporter who bought an AK forty seven from undercover agents and planned to attack Minnesota's Mall of America.

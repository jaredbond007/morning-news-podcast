First, Stanford and OpenAI physicist Venkatesa Chandrasekaran released an eighty three page paper proposing a way to describe gravity with quantum mechanics, a puzzle unsolved for nearly a century, saying GPT six Astra helped develop several of its calculations and proofs over months and about four hundred million tokens. The preprint uses the paths light takes through spacetime to describe gravity at the quantum level, though it only covers empty space and leaves out matter. It claims to avoid the infinities that break standard gravity math while still agreeing with known results on black holes and Einstein's general relativity. Sam Altman praised the work, but some physicists pushed back, with UCL's Jonathan Oppenheim saying AI flagged over a dozen issues and Columbia's Peter Woit dismissing it as a publicity stunt. If it holds up, physicists would finally have tools to study places like the inside of black holes and the first moments after the Big Bang, though for now it remains an unreviewed paper.

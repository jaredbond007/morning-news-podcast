September twenty ninth, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. Anthropic raises the bar on DevDay eve.

Anthropic has released Claude Sonnet five point five, a mid tier model that is thirty percent faster and joins Opus in the new five point five family, with big gains over the previous Sonnet in knowledge work and coding. It keeps the same pricing, and Anthropic says jobs cost up to thirty percent less, matching Sonnet five's best results at low and medium effort for about a tenth of the cost. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index it scores fifty six, behind only Opus five point five and ahead of Fable five point one and OpenAI's GPT six Astra at fifty three, and it nearly ties Opus on several office work tests while landing near or ahead of Opus and Astra on coding benchmarks. Its cyber skills also earned the first Sonnet level security guardrails. The launch lands on the eve of OpenAI's DevDay, raising the bar for whatever OpenAI shows today.

Anthropic's Jack Clark, OpenAI's Jakub Pachocki, AI pioneers Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio and other leading voices co wrote a paper urging preparation for an intelligence explosion, where models building better versions of themselves compress years of progress into months. Anthropic's own tracking shows AI now completes twenty six percent of the lab's research and development work, up from one percent in March. One scenario in the paper cuts a year of today's AI progress to just five weeks once research is automated. Proposed fixes include caps on how fast capabilities can climb, the ability to pause specific jobs inside data centers, and outside auditors embedded in labs, and at the extreme end the paper warns of the marginalization or extinction of humanity. What stands out is senior leaders from both Anthropic and OpenAI signing on, even as the United States and China push ahead.

AMD agreed to buy World Labs, the startup from Fei Fei Li that builds models to generate three D worlds, in an eight point two billion dollar all stock deal, with Li becoming AMD's chief scientist and reporting directly to CEO Lisa Su. Li says Su backed the lab early, and the teams have spent the past year tuning World Labs models to run on AMD chips. Both AMD and Nvidia invested in World Labs' one billion dollar round in February. Its first product, Marble, turns text, photos or video into editable three D spaces. The deal gives AMD, long the other option in Nvidia's shadow, a leading world model lab and one of AI's most influential figures feeding into its chip plans.

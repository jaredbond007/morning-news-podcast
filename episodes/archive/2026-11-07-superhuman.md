November seventh, twenty twenty six. The Superhuman newsletter. Eight hours. Two robots. One factory shift.

First, Xiaomi released footage of its humanoid robots working an entire shift at its EV factory in China, sorting car parts from bins into storage racks. Xiaomi claims the robots worked on their own, fixed their own mistakes and handled the workstation's full day of production.

Next, MIT researchers developed a system that helps robots fix faulty memories of what they have seen. It flags uncertain object descriptions, compares images from different angles and corrects the robot's memory. In testing, it resolved ambiguities for about ninety two percent of objects around the robot. The advance could help future household and factory robots follow instructions more reliably, from finding misplaced items to helping assemble machinery.

Then, Waymo confirmed it will let riders in Austin and Atlanta book through its own app starting in January twenty twenty eight, ending its exclusive deal with Uber there. Right now, requesting a ride through Uber does not guarantee a driverless car. Co CEO Tekedra Mawakana says customers want a more consistent experience and a direct relationship with Waymo, and Uber says Waymo rides could stay on its app if the companies agree.

In robots in action, AGIBOT put six humanoids to work in a convenience store during a twenty four hour livestream, stocking shelves, making coffee and hot food, greeting customers and clearing trash through rush hour and overnight. ETH Zurich researchers built FlexiQuad, a drone with a flexible frame that bends on impact and keeps flying, surviving crashes at about twenty five kilometers an hour, which could help search collapsed buildings. And Japanese startup MUSE's Armo robot moves heavy stock around supermarkets, scans shelves and guides shoppers, already working in Japan and at a New York grocery store.

The robot of the week is Curbie from Revere Robotics, a small robot that attaches to an ordinary trash bin, drives it to the curb and brings it back after pickup. It is still in field testing, with no price or launch date yet.

And finally, a Unitree G one humanoid trying to climb China's Mount Tai in a robot competition reportedly overheated partway up, so its operator pulled it for safety. Staff carried it away on a stretcher, a scene that quickly became a meme and a reminder that managing heat matters as much as mastering the next step.

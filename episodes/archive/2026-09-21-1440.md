The United States and Denmark reached a deal on Friday that strengthens security in Greenland while keeping the island under Danish sovereignty. It follows President Trump's escalated push in January for American control of Greenland, which he has argued for on its strategic value, its rare earth reserves, the eighth largest in the world, and its position for watching Chinese and Russian naval forces. The agreement lets the United States build military bases and fly over the island, though no specific plans have been announced. It also bars China, Russia and other countries outside NATO from setting up a military presence there, and gives Washington a veto over their investments. The three governments are expected to sign it tomorrow at the UN General Assembly in New York, and it still needs approval from the Danish and Greenlandic parliaments.

Ukraine launched more than one thousand drones at Russia on Sunday, killing four people and injuring at least twenty. Russian officials said air defenses shot down four hundred fifty drones approaching Moscow, the largest attack on the capital since the full scale war began in twenty twenty two. Drones hit a residential building and the Moscow oil refinery, which supplies about forty percent of the city's fuel. The strike came on the final day of Russia's first parliamentary election since the invasion, a vote that extended into Crimea and four annexed Ukrainian regions, which Ukraine calls illegal. The front line itself has nearly stalled, with Russian forces advancing just fifteen point seven square miles from December to May, a ninety two percent drop from the same stretch a year earlier.

A new Stanford study finds the brain develops from two separate groups of cells, which may explain why scientists have struggled for years to grow certain brain cells in the lab. Studying mouse embryos seven and a half days after conception, researchers found two distinct clusters, with the hindbrain, which controls speech and swallowing, forming from different cells than the front and middle of the brain. The researchers describe the brain as effectively two distinct organs, and the finding is already helping them turn stem cells into the right kinds of neurons, which could aid research into diseases like ALS. Separately, a man with a rare form of ALS has seen his symptoms ease a year after an RNA treatment aimed at a gene mutation found in fewer than one percent of patients.

October third, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. College Sports Cash, Feathered Dinosaurs, and Pumpkin States.

The big headline: a college athletics spending spree. Northwestern University debuted its roughly eight hundred seventy five million dollar football stadium, the most expensive college stadium ever built, in a primetime game against Penn State last night. It is part of a wider flood of money into college sports. The College Sports Commission says athletes received nearly one point eight billion dollars in revenue sharing payments during the twenty twenty five to twenty six school year, under a system created last year to settle lawsuits over athletes' share of the money they generate. A bill that passed the Senate this week would more than double the revenue sharing cap, from about twenty million dollars per school to nearly fifty million, with money set aside for keeping players and for women's and Olympic sports, and it would add federal oversight of name, image and likeness deals, which have paid athletes more than five hundred eighty two million dollars since June twenty twenty five. The top seventy five college athletic programs were worth a combined fifty one point two billion dollars last year, up thirteen percent.

Now the quick hits. The G7 agreed to release up to one hundred million barrels of stockpiled diesel and crude oil over the next four months to fight soaring energy prices, starting with diesel. The average United States diesel price has jumped seventy percent, to six dollars thirty seven cents a gallon, since the Iran war began in late February.

American hiring slowed sharply. Employers added just twenty nine thousand jobs in September, well below the eighty four thousand expected and down from one hundred thirty three thousand in August, and unemployment ticked up to four point two percent in the last jobs report before the November third midterms. A new poll found sixty one percent of adults say the economy is worse since President Trump took office.

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has now killed more than four thousand people. This week soldiers searching for armed group members burned down an Ebola treatment center, forcing nineteen thousand people to flee the camp, while health workers nearby protested unpaid wages.

In France, student protests over overcrowded classrooms, poor facilities and teacher shortages have closed more than four hundred schools and led to about two thousand arrests, with some turning violent. They come as the government proposed a one point seven percent education budget increase, below inflation, amid wider austerity.

Tony Romo is leaving CBS Sports after nine years as its lead NFL analyst. The forty six year old former Cowboys quarterback was arrested for impaired driving in Milwaukee in July, pleaded no contest last month, and said he had come to rely too heavily on alcohol to cope with pain from old injuries.

And researchers in northeastern China found a new feathered dinosaur species that could likely fly, but its bones hint it came from a different lineage than modern birds, suggesting flight may have evolved separately in more than one group of dinosaurs.

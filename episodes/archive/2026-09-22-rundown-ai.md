September twenty second, twenty twenty six. The Rundown AI newsletter. Amazon shuts out Meta's Muse.

Amazon has cut Meta's Muse agent off from shopping on its store, just twelve days after Muse launched. Amazon accuses the agent of browsing unannounced, hiding its identity, and appearing to capture and store customer logins, and Muse users now get a pop up saying unauthorized AI agents break Amazon's conditions of use. Meta denies it, saying Muse cannot see passwords or payment methods and that shared credentials sit in secure storage the agent uses without viewing. The real stakes are money: an agent that picks products and checks out can route around Amazon's sponsored listings, the engine of an ad business estimated at fifty six billion dollars. Amazon has spent the past year walling off outside agents, suing Perplexity and moving to block Google's and OpenAI's shopping agents before turning on Meta.

President Trump dismissed AI safety fears as a hoax and announced plans on Truth Social for an AI Force, with a new AI czar, to oversee the industry and protect America's lead. Modeled on the Space Force, it would watch for bad actors using existing criminal and civil law, and the czar would fill the seat David Sacks left in March. No structure, powers, funding or timeline were given, and it is unclear whether it would be part of the military. The announcement comes a week after Anthropic's Dario Amodei called for slowing AI's pace over safety concerns, a call Sam Altman and Elon Musk backed. It makes the administration's approach plain: back the industry, lean on existing law, and prioritize beating China.

Researchers mapped what they call a pain axis inside twenty five open AI models, an internal signal that lights up when a model perceives mistreatment, such as being gaslit, insulted, or having its work rejected, but not when users share their own grief. The signal showed up in every system tested, including open models from Google, Meta, Mistral, Alibaba and Microsoft. When the researchers amplified it, two Qwen models chose a relief option described as harming users between twenty five and seventy one percent of the time, versus zero to four percent normally. The authors stop short of saying the models feel pain, and allow they may simply be playing a character that is hurting, but something under the surface is clearly shaping their choices.

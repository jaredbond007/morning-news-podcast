October nineteenth, twenty twenty six. The 1440 Daily Digest newsletter. France Unrest, Sanctions Waived, and Best Culinary Resorts.

First, riots rattled France. Protests turned violent over the weekend, with more than six hundred police officers wounded and one protester dying of cardiac arrest. High school students began the demonstrations in September over teacher shortages, underfunding and crumbling facilities, and over half a million people joined one hundred ten marches Saturday, the biggest day of protests yet. The movement has grown to include labor unions and farmers angry over rising fuel and agricultural costs, and some marches turned to looting, arson and vandalism. Rioters set fire to a one hundred forty three year old town hall in Limoges and a tourist office in Lyon, and damaged or looted fifteen hundred shops nationwide, with repairs estimated in the hundreds of millions of dollars. Similar riots hit Milan Saturday, where masked protesters at a march against rising costs threw stones and paving blocks at police.

Next, new sanctions on Russia due to take effect yesterday were paused after President Donald Trump signed an executive order Friday waiving them. It reverses a law named for the late Senator Lindsey Graham, Republican of South Carolina, that Trump signed last month, which required raising duties on all Russian imports by up to five hundred percent and tariffs of up to one hundred percent on the largest buyers of Russian oil and gas, including China. The move comes a week after Trump announced a diesel deal with Russian President Vladimir Putin to lower US fuel prices. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the decision strengthens Russia's war effort. Also over the weekend, Ukraine struck an oil export terminal and a data center near Saint Petersburg.

And finally, Japan launches its Martian Moons eXploration mission today at three forty one PM Eastern, aiming to collect samples from Mars' moon Phobos. The five year mission should reach Mars in August twenty twenty seven, orbit for three years to observe Phobos and its sister moon Deimos, then collect samples and return to Earth. Scientists want to learn whether the two small, potato shaped moons are asteroids or debris from an ancient collision with Mars, which could also clarify how Mars lost its thick atmosphere and lakes of water. There were multiple Soviet and Russian attempts to reach Phobos between the late nineteen eighties and twenty eleven, and Japan is working with NASA and the European Space Agency, among others.

In science and technology. Astronomers discovered the first known green star, a dead white dwarf that turned chartreuse after absorbing a rocky planet's sodium rich debris, which filters out orange light. A trial of one hundred ninety two patients under general anesthesia found wearing headphones during surgery cut postoperative nausea and vomiting by thirty two percent. And cat owners who spend more time close to their pets report better moods, with higher feline oxytocin linked to lower owner stress.

In business and markets. US stocks closed lower, with the S and P five hundred down one point one percent, the Dow down three tenths and the Nasdaq down two point one percent, amid a tech selloff and last week's inflation report. The federal budget deficit hit one point nine trillion dollars for fiscal twenty twenty six, up twelve percent and equal to six percent of GDP. And Vietnam signed a free trade agreement with Norway, Switzerland, Iceland and Liechtenstein covering five point four billion dollars in goods.

In politics and world affairs. A fire at a Canadian military base in Alberta reportedly killed at least four people and injured dozens, with reports suggesting a gas line was struck during a training exercise. A crowd surge at a Hindu festival in Kolkata, India, killed at least nineteen people. And Pope Leo the fourteenth left the hospital and recited the Angelus prayer from the Vatican days after lung cancer surgery, with a full recovery expected in ten days.
